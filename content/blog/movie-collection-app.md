## What should a movie collection app do?

Before comparing apps, it helps to know what separates a good one from a glorified list. Collectors on forums like r/dvdcollection and AV Nirvana raise the same handful of needs again and again:

:::cards
### Scan, don't type
The [UPC or EAN barcode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Product_Code) on the case identifies the exact release. An app that reads it fills in title, year, format and cover in seconds.
### Track editions, not just films
Your *Dune* 4K steelbook and your old Blu-ray are two different things. A good app keeps them separate.
### Work in the store
The whole point is checking whether you already own something before you buy it again. That means fast search on your phone.
### Handle box sets
A trilogy set should be one item on your shelf but still show up when you search for any film inside it.
### Let you leave
Export matters. If you ever switch apps, you want your list to come with you.
:::

:::pullquote
The best catalog is the one you open in the store before you buy the same movie twice.
:::

## Which movie collection app is best for you?

Each of these apps is good at something. The right pick depends on how big your collection is, what formats you own and how you feel about subscriptions.

### My Movies by Blu-ray.com: best free option

If you already use Blu-ray.com to read reviews or check release dates, this is the obvious starting point. The app is free, membership is free, and you can scan a barcode to add a disc to your collection, then filter it by format and other attributes. You also get Blu-ray.com's deals, release calendar and a calendar of collectible editions like steelbooks.

The trade-off is that your collection lives inside Blu-ray.com's ecosystem, and the app is built around their database rather than around your shelves.

### CLZ Movies: best for deep data and desktop editing

CLZ has been making collection software for a long time, and it shows. The app pulls cast, crew and plot details from IMDb, supports custom fields and multiple collections, and syncs through CLZ Cloud so you can also manage your list from a computer.

It is subscription-only: US $1.99 a month or $19.99 a year after a 7-day trial, according to CLZ's own iOS help manual. Collectors who dislike recurring fees tend to mention that first.

### iCollect Movies: best for mixed and vintage formats

If your shelves include VHS tapes, LaserDiscs, HD DVDs or even CED discs, iCollect Movies covers more physical formats than any other app here. It offers cloud backup and sync across iPhone, iPad and Mac, fields for purchase price and storage location, and imports from CLZ Movies and Letterboxd.

Pricing has several layers: one-time unlimited unlocks, a yearly Pro subscription for bulk editing, and separately sold packs of AI identification scans. Read the in-app purchase list before you commit.

### My Movies 6: best for digital plus physical

My Movies 6 tracks physical discs alongside digital copies and includes a wish list, with batch barcode scanning. You need an account, and your collection syncs through the developer's servers, which doubles as a backup. The free version stops at 50 titles, with several upgrade options after that.

### Movieshelf: best for editions, box sets and shelf locations

Movieshelf is built for collectors who care about *which* copy they own and *where* it is. You can scan up to 10 barcodes in one pass and import them together, track DVD, Blu-ray and 4K UHD editions separately, store box sets as one package with every film inside, sort discs into custom folders and record a shelf location for each one. A watchlist and "what to watch" picks help you actually use what you own, and collection stats show how it breaks down.

The free plan covers 50 titles and 50 barcode scans, with unlimited folders and 3 custom fields. Pro unlocks unlimited adding and scanning, unlimited custom fields, collection analytics, custom posters and backdrops, and bulk CSV import and export. It costs $2.99 a month, $14.99 a year, or $44.99 once for lifetime access. There's no free trial, so the free plan is the way to try it. Full details are on the [pricing section](/#pricing).

Movieshelf is an iPhone app, so if you need Android or a desktop editor, CLZ or iCollect is a better fit.

## Is a spreadsheet good enough?

Plenty of collectors run a perfectly happy spreadsheet. It's free, it never locks you in, and you can add any column you want. The question is whether you'll keep it current.

:::versus Spreadsheet | Collection app
- Free and fully flexible
- You type every title by hand
- No covers unless you paste them in
- Awkward to search on a phone in a store
---
- Scan a barcode and the details fill in
- Covers and formats pulled in automatically
- Built for quick search on your phone
- Often paid once you pass a free limit
:::

A good rule of thumb: under about 100 discs and no in-store shopping, a spreadsheet works. Beyond that, the time saved by scanning adds up fast. If you're moving an existing list over, an app with an import option (Movieshelf Pro takes a CSV file) saves you re-entering everything. ## How to choose and set up your app in one evening

Don't overthink the choice. Install two candidates, run the same small test on both, and keep the one that feels faster.

:::steps
### Pick five tricky discs
Grab a steelbook, a box set, an import, a 4K combo pack and something old. These are where apps differ.
### Scan all five in each app
Check whether the right edition and format come up, not just the right film.
### Try a search like you're in a store
Type half a title and see how fast you get an answer.
### Check the exit door
Find the export option before you add 500 discs, so you know your data can leave with you.
### Commit and scan a shelf a night
Pick one app, then work through your collection one shelf at a time.
:::

{{phone:collection}}

Once your discs are in, give each one a shelf address so the app can tell you exactly where it lives. Our guide to [organizing a DVD collection](/blog/how-to-organize-dvd-collection/) compares nine shelving systems that pair well with a catalog.

:::checklist Before you commit to a movie collection app
- It scans the barcode on a steelbook and a standard edition as two different items
- Box sets can be stored as one package
- Search is fast enough to use in a store aisle
- You know what the free limit is and what happens after it
- You can export your collection to a file
- You're comfortable with the price model: free, subscription or one-time
:::

:::tip Scanning tips that work in any app
Tilt glossy cases and slipcovers away from overhead lights, since glare causes most misreads. If a sticker is torn, type the digits under the bars instead.
:::
