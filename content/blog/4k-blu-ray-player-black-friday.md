### Your TV has Dolby Vision
LG, Sony, Panasonic and TCL TVs often do. A DP-UB450 or DP-UB820 lets you see the Dolby Vision layer on discs that have it.
### Your TV is a Samsung
Samsung TVs don't support Dolby Vision. Save money with the DP-UB150, which handles HDR10 and HDR10+.

A PS5 or Xbox Series X already plays 4K discs in HDR10. Only upgrade if you want Dolby Vision or a quieter, faster player.

The DP-UB820's second, audio-only HDMI output makes it the easiest fit with an older receiver.

## Is a "4K upscaling" Blu-ray player a 4K Blu-ray player?

No, and this is the most common Black Friday mistake. Doorbuster ads often show a "4K upscaling Blu-ray player" for well under $100. These players play DVDs and regular Blu-rays and scale the picture up to a 4K signal. They **cannot play 4K UHD discs at all**.

:::versus 4K UHD Blu-ray player | 4K upscaling Blu-ray player
- Plays 4K UHD discs, Blu-rays and DVDs
- Native 3840×2160 picture from 4K discs
- HDR10, and Dolby Vision on most Panasonic models
- Usually $200 and up
---
- Plays Blu-rays and DVDs only
- Stretches a 1080p picture to 4K output
- No HDR from disc
- Often under $100 on sale
:::

The giveaway is in the name. A real 4K player says "Ultra HD Blu-ray" or "4K UHD Blu-ray playback." If the listing only says "4K upscaling," your new 4K steelbooks won't play in it. Our explainer on [upscaling 4K Blu-ray players](/blog/upscaling-4k-blu-ray-player/) covers when one actually makes sense.

:::warning Check the model number twice
Sony's cheaper Blu-ray players and many no-name brands use "4K" prominently on the box. Search the exact model number with "UHD" before you check out.
:::

## Should you wait for a better deal after Black Friday?

For players, waiting is a gamble that rarely pays this year. Three things point the same way:

- **Prices went up, not down.** Panasonic raised list prices in 2026, and nothing suggests they'll fall back.
- **The market is shrinking.** Sony has stopped making its standalone 4K player, the UBP-X700, so what's left is remaining stock. Panasonic is now the main maker of mainstream models.
- **Holiday sales are the reliable window.** Black Friday through Cyber Monday is when retailers most consistently discount home theater gear.

:::pullquote
In a shrinking market, the best Black Friday deal is the player you actually buy, at a price you're happy with.
:::

If you play imports, remember that US players are locked to Region A for regular Blu-rays (4K UHD discs are almost all region free). A [region-free Blu-ray player](/blog/best-region-free-blu-ray-player/) is a different purchase, and it's rarely part of mainstream sales.

## How do you plan a Black Friday disc wishlist without buying duplicates?

A new player usually means new discs, and Black Friday disc sales move fast. The collectors who do well have a list ready before the sales start, and they know what's already on their shelf. Here's a simple plan:

:::steps
### Catalog what you already own
Scan the barcodes of your 4K and Blu-ray shelf first. You can't avoid duplicates if you don't know exactly which editions you have, especially for films with several releases.
### Write the wishlist by edition, not by title
"*Dune: Part Two*, 4K steelbook" is a wishlist entry. "*Dune*" isn't. Many films have a 4K, a Blu-ray, a steelbook and a boutique edition, and they're priced very differently.
### Mark which titles are 4K upgrades
If you own the Blu-ray, the 4K is an upgrade, not a new purchase. Decide in advance which upgrades are worth it for the picture and which aren't.
### Set a price ceiling for each disc
Pick the most you'll pay for each title. If a deal doesn't beat it, skip it. This stops "it's on sale" from becoming the reason you buy.
### Check your collection before you pay
In a store or a checkout queue, search your collection or scan the barcode. Ten seconds beats a duplicate you can't return.
:::

## What should be on your Black Friday checklist?

:::checklist Black Friday checklist for disc collectors
- Check whether your TV supports Dolby Vision, HDR10+ or both.
- Note the 2026 list price of the player you want.
- Confirm the listing says "Ultra HD Blu-ray," not just "4K upscaling."
- Buy from the retailer itself, not a third-party seller.
- Catalog your current shelf before the disc sales start.
- Write your disc wishlist by exact edition, with a price ceiling for each.
- Search or scan your collection before every disc purchase.
- Log new discs the day they arrive.
:::
