**To fix a scratched DVD, start by cleaning it properly and testing it in a second player, because most "scratched" discs that skip are actually dirty. If it still fails, polish light scratches out with a disc polish or a mild abrasive, rubbing in straight lines from the center to the edge, or take it to a resurfacing service.** Blu-rays and 4K UHD discs are different: their data sits just under a hard coating, so gentle cleaning and replacement beat DIY polishing.

The rest of this guide shows you how to tell a scratch that matters from one that doesn't, which home remedies actually do something, and when a disc is better replaced than repaired.

:::stats
- 0.6 mm | of plastic over a DVD's data | room to polish a scratch out
- 0.1 mm | over a Blu-ray's data | why DIY repair is risky
- 1st | thing to try is cleaning | most skips are dirt, not damage
:::

<div class="tldr">

**TL;DR**

1. Clean the disc first, wiping center to edge. That alone fixes a lot of skipping.
2. Try it in another player, console or computer drive.
3. For a DVD or CD, polish light scratches in straight lines from the center out, or pay for resurfacing.
4. For a Blu-ray or 4K disc, clean gently and replace it if it still fails.
5. Cracks, label-side holes on CDs and disc rot can't be fixed by any method.

</div>

## Is it really a scratch, or something else?

Before you rub anything into a disc, work out what's wrong with it. Hold it under a bright light, read side up, then tilt it and look through it toward the light.

| What you see or hear | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Smudges, haze, a sticky film | Dirt, fingerprints, residue | Clean it. No repair needed. |
| Fine lines you can catch with light but not with a fingernail | Light surface scratches | Usually plays fine. Leave it, or polish gently if it skips. |
| A groove your fingernail catches on | Deep scratch | Polish or professional resurfacing (DVDs and CDs only). |
| Pinholes of light shining through | Damaged metal layer, often CD label-side damage | Can't be repaired. Replace it. |
| Bronze, cloudy or speckled patches inside the disc | Disc rot | Can't be repaired. See our [disc rot guide](/blog/disc-rot/). |
| A crack from the center hole | Physical crack | Stop playing it. A cracked disc can shatter in a fast drive. |

If the disc only *looks* bad but plays all the way through, the honest advice from long-time collectors is to leave it alone. Every repair method removes plastic, and you can't put it back.

:::tip Clean before you judge
Wipe the disc with a soft cloth and a little distilled water, in straight lines from the center out, and test it again. Our guide to [cleaning DVDs and Blu-rays](/blog/how-to-clean-dvds/) has the full method. Plenty of "ruined" thrift-store discs are simply greasy.
:::

## Which scratches actually make a DVD skip?

Not all scratches are equal. A disc's data runs in one long spiral track from the center outward, and your player's error correction can fill in small gaps. What matters is the direction of the scratch.

The [NIST care guide for librarians and archivists](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/NISTspecialpublication500-252.pdf) says scratches that run along the track are worse, while scratches running outward from the center have a good chance of being covered by error correction.

:::versus Straight scratch, center to edge | Curved scratch, around the disc
- Crosses each loop of the track briefly
- Error correction usually fills the gaps
- Often harmless even when it looks ugly
---
- Follows the track for a long stretch
- Wipes out a continuous run of data
- The classic cause of a disc that freezes in one spot
:::

The other factor is which side is damaged. On a CD, the data sits just under the thin label, so NIST notes that a scratch or pinhole on the *label* side can destroy the data and can't be repaired. On a single-sided DVD, the data sits in the middle of the disc, so label-side scratches are rarely a problem.

## How do you fix a scratched DVD step by step?

This sequence goes from zero risk to some risk. Stop at the first step that works.

:::steps
### Clean it properly
Rinse off grit under lukewarm water, then wipe the read side with a soft cotton or microfiber cloth from the center out. Let it dry fully.
### Test it in another player
Drives differ in how well they read marginal discs. Try a different DVD player, a game console or a computer drive. If it plays elsewhere, the disc may be fine and your player's laser may need attention.
### Find the problem spot
Note the chapter or time where it skips. A scratch near that point (discs are read from the center outward) is your target. You don't need to polish the whole disc.
### Polish light scratches from the center out
Use a disc polish or scratch-repair kit on a soft cloth, or a tiny amount of plain white paste toothpaste. Rub in straight lines from the center to the edge with light pressure, never in circles, for a minute or two.
### Wash off every trace of polish
Rinse under lukewarm water, dry with a clean soft cloth, and check against the light. Residue left in the case or the player causes new problems.
### Test, then repeat once at most
If it's better but not fixed, one more light pass is reasonable. If it's no better, move on to professional resurfacing or a replacement.
:::

:::warning Keep it off the label and the hub
Polish only the shiny read side. Keep abrasives and alcohol away from the printed label, and never sand, scrape or press hard near the center hole, where most cracks start.
:::

## Do toothpaste and other home remedies actually work?

Search for "how to fix a scratched DVD" and you'll find a dozen kitchen-cupboard tricks. They fall into two groups. **Abrasives** (toothpaste, polish, a Magic Eraser) remove plastic until the scratch is shallower. **Fillers** (wax, petroleum jelly, lip balm) fill the scratch so the laser passes through it. Here's an honest verdict on each.

:::cards
### Disc polish or repair kit
The most controlled DIY option for DVDs and CDs. Made for polycarbonate, with a fine grit. Results vary with scratch depth.
### Plain white toothpaste
A mild abrasive that can help light scratches. Gel and whitening pastes are a gamble. Collectors report mixed results and some haze.
### Magic Eraser
A dry melamine sponge is a fine abrasive. It can work on light scratches, but it's easy to overdo and leave a dull patch.
### Wax, petroleum jelly, lip balm
Fillers, not fixes. They may get you one more viewing, but they wipe off, attract dust and can leave residue in your player.
### Heat from a lamp
Skip it. Polycarbonate can warp, and a warped disc won't play anywhere.
### Sandpaper and metal polish
Only with real experience and an expendable disc. Too coarse a grit, and you'll cut deeper scratches than you started with.
:::

:::pullquote
Every scratch fix works by removing plastic, so the best repair is the smallest one that gets the disc playing.
:::

If your computer can read a skipping disc after a quick polish, that's also a good moment to watch the film through and decide whether this copy is a keeper or a disc to replace.

## How do you fix a scratched Blu-ray or 4K disc?

Gently, and often not at all. The Blu-ray format puts its data much closer to the surface than DVD does, so it requires a hard, scratch-resistant coating over the read side. TDK's [Durabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durabis) coating was one of the first, and studios' pressed discs use similar hard coats.

:::bars How much plastic sits over the data on the read side
- CD | 1.2 | ~1.2 mm
- DVD | 0.6 | ~0.6 mm
- Blu-ray and 4K UHD | 0.1 | ~0.1 mm
:::

Samsung's [disc format comparison](https://www.samsung.com/in/support/tv-audio-video/what-is-the-difference-between-blu-ray-disc-dvd-and-cd/) lists the 0.6 mm DVD and 0.1 mm Blu-ray protective layers. That thin layer cuts both ways. The hard coat means a Blu-ray shrugs off marks that would hurt a DVD, but once a scratch is through it, there is very little plastic left to polish.

That's why some game shops and resurfacing services won't take Blu-ray discs at all, and why forum advice from Blu-ray collectors is consistent: clean it, try another player, and replace a badly scratched copy rather than polishing it. If you do try anything, use only a disc cleaner made for Blu-ray and a soft cloth, center to edge, with almost no pressure.

:::note 4K UHD discs
4K UHD discs use the same thin-cover design as Blu-ray, with up to three data layers. Treat them like Blu-rays: clean gently and avoid abrasives entirely.
:::

## Is professional disc resurfacing worth it?

Resurfacing machines spin the disc against a polishing wheel and remove a thin, even layer of the read side, which is far more consistent than doing it by hand. Some used-media stores, game shops and libraries offer it, and dedicated disc repair services take mail-in orders.

:::versus Worth resurfacing | Not worth it
- Rare or out-of-print DVDs and CDs
- Discs from a box set you can't buy singly
- Discs with read-side scratches only
- A disc that has never been resurfaced before
---
- Common films you can replace for a few dollars
- Blu-ray and 4K UHD discs
- Cracks, label-side holes or disc rot
- Discs that already show swirl marks from earlier resurfacing
:::

Two cautions. First, resurfacing removes material every time, so a disc can only be done a limited number of times. Second, quality varies: collectors report discs coming back with haze or ripples. Ask whether the shop takes your disc type and what happens if it doesn't play afterward.

## When should you replace a disc instead of fixing it?

Sometimes the right fix is a new copy, often in a better format. Run through this before you spend time or money on repair:

:::checklist Repair or replace?
- The disc **still plays** all the way through: leave it alone.
- It's a **common title**: check the price of a used copy or a Blu-ray upgrade first.
- It's a **Blu-ray or 4K disc** with a deep scratch: replace it.
- You see **pinholes, cracks or rot**: replace it, no repair will help.
- It's **rare or out of print**: try careful polishing, then professional resurfacing.
- It's part of a **box set**: search for a single replacement disc before rebuying the set.
:::

Whichever way you go, write it down. A disc that freezes at 1:12:40 is easy to forget until movie night, and buyers ask about condition first if you ever [sell DVDs for money](/blog/sell-dvds-for-money/).

## How do you stop discs getting scratched again?

Most scratches happen in the few seconds between case and player. These habits prevent nearly all of them:

- Hold discs **by the edge and center hole** only.
- Put every disc **straight back in its case**, never face-down on a table.
- Replace cases with **broken hubs**, which let discs rattle loose inside.
- Don't stack bare discs or carry them in paper sleeves for long.
- Keep cases **upright and closed** on the shelf.

## Frequently asked questions

{{faq}}

:::takeaways
- **Clean first.** Dirt causes more skipping than scratches do.
- **Direction matters.** Straight scratches are usually harmless, curved ones cause freezes.
- **DVDs and CDs can be polished**, always in straight lines from the center out.
- **Blu-rays and 4K discs:** clean gently, then replace. Don't polish.
- **Cracks, CD label-side holes and disc rot** can't be repaired.

## Fix the easy ones tonight

Pull out the discs that have ever skipped on you, clean them, and test them again. You'll probably rescue half of them in ten minutes. Note the ones that still fail, decide repair or replace, and your shelf will be ready for movie night.
