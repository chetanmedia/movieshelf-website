**An upscaling 4K Blu-ray player is a regular Blu-ray player that stretches HD and DVD discs to 2160p for a 4K TV; it cannot play 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray discs.** Every 4K TV already upscales on its own, so a player that only upscales adds little today. If you want 4K discs, you need a UHD player or a console with a UHD drive, and those upscale your older discs too.

The question most collectors are really asking is whether they need a *dedicated* 4K player at all, or whether a PS5, an Xbox or the TV can do the job. Here's how upscaling works, where it should happen in your setup, and when a dedicated player is worth the money.

:::stats
- 4× | the pixels of a 4K frame versus a 1080p Blu-ray | 3840 × 2160 vs 1920 × 1080
- 24× | the pixels a DVD has to be stretched to fill 4K | from 720 × 480 (NTSC)
- 0 | 4K UHD discs an "upscaling" Blu-ray player can read | it's a regular Blu-ray drive
:::

<div class="tldr">

**TL;DR**

- **"4K upscaling" ≠ 4K playback.** Players like Sony's BDP-S6700 play Blu-ray and DVD only. They can't read UHD discs.
- **Your 4K TV upscales already.** An upscaling-only player is worth it only if it's nearly free and you never plan to buy 4K discs.
- **Consoles are a fine start.** A disc PS5 or Xbox Series X plays UHD discs in HDR10. No Dolby Vision or HDR10+ from disc on either.
- **Go dedicated** when you own lots of DVDs and Blu-rays, have a Dolby Vision TV, or watch on a big screen or projector.

## What does an upscaling 4K Blu-ray player actually do?

Upscaling takes a picture with fewer pixels and resizes it to fill a screen with more. A Blu-ray holds 1920 × 1080 pixels per frame. A 4K TV has 3840 × 2160, four times as many. Something in the chain has to invent the missing three-quarters of every frame, and how well it guesses decides whether the result looks crisp or soft and noisy.

:::bars Pixels per frame, in millions
- DVD (720 × 480) | 0.35 | 0.35 million
- Blu-ray (1920 × 1080) | 2.07 | 2.07 million
- 4K UHD (3840 × 2160) | 8.29 | 8.29 million
:::

A box sold as a "Blu-ray player with 4K upscaling" does that resizing before the signal reaches your TV. What it doesn't have is an Ultra HD Blu-ray drive. The [Ultra HD Blu-ray format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray) needs different hardware, and it also brings HDR, which no amount of upscaling can add to an SDR Blu-ray.

:::versus Upscaling Blu-ray player | 4K UHD Blu-ray player
- Plays Blu-ray, DVD and CD
- Outputs 2160p by resizing HD and SD video
- No HDR from disc, because Blu-rays don't carry it
- Can't read 4K UHD discs at all
- Usually cheaper, and getting harder to find new
---
- Plays 4K UHD, Blu-ray, DVD and CD
- Native 2160p from 4K discs, with HDR10 at minimum
- Upscales your Blu-rays and DVDs too
- Dolby Vision and HDR10+ on many models
- The only way to watch the 4K editions on your shelf
:::

:::warning Read the box carefully
"4K upscaling," "4K ready" and "4K output" on a player's box don't mean it plays 4K discs. Look for **Ultra HD Blu-ray** or the UHD logo. If it isn't there, the player is HD only.
:::

## Where should upscaling happen: player, TV or receiver?

Your signal can be upscaled by the player, an AV receiver, or the TV. Only one of them should do it, and it should be the one with the best processor. Stacking scalers just adds softness and delay.

The answer used to be "the player," because early 4K TVs had weak processing. That's no longer a safe assumption. In [What Hi-Fi's console test](https://www.whathifi.com/advice/which-is-the-best-4k-blu-ray-player-ps5-or-xbox-series-x), the Xbox Series X upscaled 1080p discs so softly that the reviewers suggested setting it to 1080p output and letting a good 4K TV do the work instead.

At the other end, [TechRadar's comparison of Panasonic's range](https://www.techradar.com/televisions/blu-ray/i-test-4k-blu-ray-players-for-a-living-and-heres-the-difference-between-premium-and-budget) found upscaling improves as you climb the line, with the DP-UB820 showing finer detail and less noise than the budget DP-UB154, and the DP-UB9000 the cleanest of all.

:::steps
### Set the player to 4K output and watch a familiar Blu-ray
Pick a disc you know well, like *The Dark Knight* or *No Country for Old Men*, and note faces, fabric and film grain.
### Switch the player to 1080p output and watch the same scene
Now the TV does the upscaling. Turn off any "noise reduction" or "sharpness" boosts on the TV first.
### Repeat with a DVD
DVDs show the biggest difference, because they need far more stretching than Blu-rays.
### Keep whichever looks more natural
Not sharper. Natural. Over-sharpened upscaling halos edges and smears grain.
:::

## Is a PS5 or Xbox a good enough 4K Blu-ray player?

For a lot of people, yes. If a console with a UHD drive is already under your TV, it's the cheapest way into 4K discs. The catch is that consoles do the basics and skip the extras collectors care about.

| Device | Plays 4K UHD discs? | Dolby Vision / HDR10+ from disc | Upscaling of Blu-ray and DVD |
|---|---|---|---|
| PS5 (disc) or PS5 + disc drive | Yes | No | Good, cleaner than Series X in What Hi-Fi's test |
| Xbox Series X | Yes | No | Softer; let a good TV upscale instead |
| Xbox One S / One X | Yes | No | Upscales to 4K; older hardware |
| PS4 Pro | No, Blu-ray and DVD only | No | Let the TV handle it |
| Xbox Series S, digital-only consoles | No disc drive | — | — |
| Blu-ray player with 4K upscaling | No | No | Varies by model |
| Panasonic DP-UB820 / DP-UB9000 | Yes | Yes, both | Very good to excellent |

The PS4 Pro surprises people. It launched in 2016 with 4K gaming but, as [Engadget reported](https://www.engadget.com/2016/09/07/ps4-pro-uhd-blu-ray), a regular Blu-ray drive. The Xbox One S, released the same year, did get a UHD drive, so a used One S is still a cheap way to play 4K discs in HDR10.

:::note Dolby Atmos works on consoles too
Both the PS5 and Xbox Series X can send Dolby Atmos to a receiver or soundbar, but setup is fussier than on a dedicated player. The PS5 needs bitstream audio turned on, and the Xbox relies on Dolby and DTS apps.
:::

## When is a dedicated 4K Blu-ray player worth it?

A dedicated player isn't just for 4K discs. It's what makes everything else on your shelf look its best, which matters a lot if most of your collection was bought before 4K existed.

:::cards
### Your shelf is mostly DVD and Blu-ray
A good player's upscaling lifts hundreds of discs at once. That's more value than any single 4K upgrade.
### You have a Dolby Vision or HDR10+ TV
Consoles play UHD discs in HDR10 only. A Panasonic DP-UB820 or higher plays both dynamic formats.
### You watch on a big screen or projector
Upscaling flaws grow with screen size. Projector owners benefit most from the DP-UB9000's processing and HDR tone mapping.
### You want quiet, fast, simple
Dedicated players load discs faster, run quieter and work with a normal remote. No controller, no sign-in, no updates mid-film.
### You buy imports
Standard US players are locked to Region A Blu-rays and Region 1 DVDs. Some dedicated players can be bought region-free; consoles can't.
### Your console is busy
If someone is gaming, nobody is watching a disc. A second box ends that argument.
:::

:::pullquote
Most collections are mostly DVD and Blu-ray, so the player's upscaling is the feature you use most, not its 4K drive.
:::

Not sure what your collection is actually made of? Count it before you shop. If 80% of your discs are DVDs, a player with excellent upscaling will change more movie nights than one with the most HDR logos.

## Which player should you choose for your setup?

Use this as a quick decision path:

- **No 4K discs, no plans to buy any, basic 4K TV:** keep your current Blu-ray player and let the TV upscale. Don't buy an upscaling-only player.
- **Curious about 4K, already own a disc PS5 or Xbox Series X:** start with the console. Buy a few 4K discs and see if you care.
- **Samsung TV (HDR10+, no Dolby Vision):** a budget Panasonic UHD player covers HDR10+ and upscales better than a console.
- **Dolby Vision TV and a growing 4K shelf:** the Panasonic DP-UB820 is the default pick.
- **Projector, big screen, or hundreds of DVDs:** the DP-UB9000 for its processing.

Our [UHD 4K Blu-ray player guide](/blog/uhd-4k-blu-ray-player/) has current models, list prices and feature tables, and [4K Blu-ray vs Blu-ray](/blog/4k-blu-ray-vs-blu-ray/) covers which films are worth the upgrade.

:::checklist Get the best upscaled picture tonight
- Pick **one** device to upscale: player or TV, not both.
- Turn off TV sharpness boosts and heavy noise reduction.
- Use your TV's Filmmaker, Cinema or Movie picture mode.
- On the player, set output to **Auto** or **4K** and test against 1080p.
- Use the HDMI port your TV labels for 4K HDR, with a Premium High Speed cable.
- Test with a DVD, a Blu-ray and a 4K disc you know well.
:::
