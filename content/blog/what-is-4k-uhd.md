**4K UHD means video at 3840×2160 pixels, four times the resolution of 1080p HD.** On a movie shelf, "4K UHD" refers to Ultra HD Blu-ray, a disc format launched in 2016 that combines that resolution with high dynamic range (HDR), 10-bit color and discs holding up to 100 GB. It needs a 4K UHD player and looks its best on a 4K HDR TV.

That's the dictionary answer. The collector's answer is more useful: what the label actually promises, what it doesn't, and how to tell whether a given 4K disc is worth owning over the Blu-ray you already have.

:::stats
- 4× | the pixels of a 1080p Blu-ray | 8.3 million vs 2.1 million
- 10-bit | color, with HDR10 on every disc | about 1.07 billion shades
- 100 GB | on a triple-layer disc | double a dual-layer Blu-ray
:::

**TL;DR**

- **UHD = 3840×2160.** "4K" on TVs and discs means the same thing; cinema 4K is slightly wider.
- **4K UHD Blu-ray** is a separate disc format from Blu-ray, with HDR, wider color and more storage.
- **You need a UHD player** (or a PS5 with a disc drive, or an Xbox Series X). Standard Blu-ray players can't read the discs.
- **HDR matters more than pixels.** Plenty of 4K discs are upscaled from 2K masters and still look clearly better.
- **The format is region-free**, but the Blu-ray in a combo pack may not be.

## What does 4K UHD mean?

"UHD" stands for Ultra High Definition. For TVs, streaming and discs, it's a resolution of 3840×2160 pixels, which is exactly twice the width and twice the height of 1080p. That's where "four times the pixels" comes from.

"4K" is borrowed from cinema. In theaters, the DCI 4K standard is 4096×2160, a slightly wider frame. Consumer electronics adopted "4K" as a marketing term for UHD, and today the two are used interchangeably. When a case says **4K Ultra HD**, the video on the disc is 3840×2160.

:::bars Pixels per frame, by disc format
- DVD (NTSC, 720×480) | 345600 | 0.35 million
- Blu-ray (1920×1080) | 2073600 | 2.1 million
- 4K UHD Blu-ray (3840×2160) | 8294400 | 8.3 million
:::

:::note UHD vs 4K in one line
UHD is 3840×2160, cinema 4K is 4096×2160, and on anything you can buy for your living room the two words mean the same thing.
:::

## What is a 4K UHD Blu-ray disc?

A 4K UHD Blu-ray (officially **Ultra HD Blu-ray**) is its own disc format, finalized by the Blu-ray Disc Association in 2015. The first discs went on sale in the US in February 2016. It looks like a Blu-ray and uses the same blue laser, but the specification is a big step up.

| | Blu-ray | 4K UHD Blu-ray |
|---|---|---|
| Resolution | 1920×1080 | 3840×2160 |
| Color depth | 8-bit | 10-bit (12-bit container for Dolby Vision) |
| Color space | Rec. 709 | Rec. 2020 (wider gamut) |
| HDR | None | HDR10 on every disc; Dolby Vision or HDR10+ optional |
| Video codec | Mostly AVC (H.264) | HEVC (H.265) |
| Disc capacity | 25 or 50 GB | 50, 66 or 100 GB |
| Region coding | A, B or C on many discs | None |
| Case | Usually blue | Usually black |

The full specification is summarized on [Wikipedia's Ultra HD Blu-ray page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_HD_Blu-ray), which is a good reference if you want the codec and data-rate details.

:::cards
### Resolution
Four times the pixels of Blu-ray. Most visible on large screens and projectors, and in films with fine texture such as landscapes, fabric and faces.
### High dynamic range
Brighter highlights and deeper shadows. Sunlight, explosions and neon finally look bright instead of flat white.
### Wider color
Rec. 2020 color and 10-bit depth mean smoother gradients, with far less banding in skies and dark scenes.
### Room to breathe
Discs up to 100 GB give the video a much higher bitrate than streaming, which shows in grain, darkness and fast motion.
:::

## Why does HDR matter more than the extra pixels?

Ask collectors on r/4kbluray what makes a 4K disc worth it and the answer is rarely "resolution." On a 55-inch TV at normal viewing distance, 1080p and 2160p are hard to tell apart. HDR is not. Specular highlights, like the glint on a car or the sun through a window, can be dramatically brighter than the rest of the image, and dark scenes keep detail instead of crushing to black.

There are three HDR formats you'll see on disc:

- **HDR10** is mandatory, so every 4K UHD disc has it. It uses static metadata: one set of brightness instructions for the whole film.
- **Dolby Vision** is optional and uses dynamic metadata, adjusting scene by scene. Your player and TV both need to support it.
- **HDR10+** is also optional and dynamic, mainly backed by Samsung and some studios. It's far less common on disc.

:::pullquote
Resolution is what the box sells. HDR is what you actually notice.
:::

## Is every 4K UHD Blu-ray true 4K?

No, and this is the part most explainers skip. Many films, especially from the 2000s and 2010s, were finished on a **2K digital intermediate**, because their visual effects were rendered at 2K. Their 4K discs are upscaled from that 2K master. The box still says 4K Ultra HD.

That doesn't make them bad discs. As [Sound & Vision points out](https://www.soundandvision.com/content/are-4k-ultra-hd-blu-rays-really-4k), *Guardians of the Galaxy Vol. 2* is reportedly sourced from a 2K intermediate and was still used as a show-off demo disc, because the HDR and color carry it.

:::versus Native 4K master | 2K master, upscaled
- Scanned or rendered at 4K or higher
- Common for older films restored from the original negative
- Gains detail and HDR over the Blu-ray
---
- Finished at 2K, upscaled for the disc
- Common for VFX-heavy films of the 2000s and 2010s
- Gains HDR and color, but little extra detail
:::

:::tip How to check before you buy
Look up the film's **Technical Specs** page on IMDb, which often lists the digital intermediate resolution, and read a disc review that covers the 4K transfer specifically. If the master is 2K and you mostly watch on a smaller TV, the Blu-ray may be all you need.
:::

## What do you need to play 4K UHD Blu-ray?

Three things, and the first one catches people out: a regular Blu-ray player won't read a UHD disc at all.

:::steps
### A UHD-capable player
A dedicated 4K UHD Blu-ray player, a PS5 with a disc drive, or an Xbox Series X (or Xbox One S / One X). The PS4 Pro and Xbox Series S can't play 4K discs.
### A 4K TV or projector with HDR
Any 4K set works, but HDR is where most of the improvement lives. For Dolby Vision discs, your TV and player both need Dolby Vision support.
### An HDCP 2.2 HDMI connection
Use an HDMI input on your TV that supports 4K HDR, usually labeled HDCP 2.2, and a high-speed cable. If you route through a soundbar or receiver, it needs 4K HDR passthrough too.
:::

If you're choosing a player, our guide to [UHD 4K Blu-ray players](/blog/uhd-4k-blu-ray-player/) covers what's still on sale and which features are worth paying for.

:::warning Got a 1080p TV?
A UHD player will downscale 4K discs for a 1080p set, but you lose the resolution and most of the HDR effect. Until you upgrade the screen, the regular Blu-ray is usually the better use of your money.
:::

## How can you tell a 4K UHD disc from a Blu-ray on your shelf?

Most of the time, the case tells you:

- **A black case** with a **4K Ultra HD** banner across the top. Standard Blu-rays are usually blue.
- **The Ultra HD Blu-ray logo** on the spine or back.
- **"4K Ultra HD + Blu-ray + Digital"** on the front for combo packs. Most major-studio 4K releases include the 1080p Blu-ray as a second disc.
- **HDR badges** (HDR10, Dolby Vision, HDR10+) and audio badges (Dolby Atmos, DTS:X) on the back.

Steelbooks and boutique releases break the color rule, so check the back of the case or the disc itself. A 4K disc is labeled Ultra HD Blu-ray on its printed side.

## Are 4K UHD Blu-rays region-free?

The Ultra HD Blu-ray format has **no region coding**, so a 4K disc bought in the UK, Germany or Japan will play on a US player, and vice versa. That makes 4K the friendliest format for importers.

The catch is the combo pack. The 1080p Blu-ray included alongside the 4K disc follows normal Blu-ray rules and can be locked to Region A, B or C. If you buy an import, check the region of the Blu-ray too. Our explainer on [Blu-ray regions A, B and C](/blog/what-blu-ray-region-is-the-us/) goes deeper.

## Is 4K UHD worth it for your collection?

For most collectors the answer depends on three things: screen size, whether your TV does HDR well, and how much the specific film benefits. Big, visually rich films with a native 4K master gain the most. Dialogue-driven comedies on a mid-size TV gain the least.

We cover that decision in detail, including which titles to upgrade first, in [4K Blu-ray vs Blu-ray: Is the Upgrade Worth It?](/blog/4k-blu-ray-vs-blu-ray/)

:::checklist Before you buy a 4K UHD disc
- Your player reads **Ultra HD Blu-ray**, not just Blu-ray.
- Your TV is **4K with HDR**, connected to an HDCP 2.2 HDMI port.
- You've checked whether the film is a **native 4K or 2K** master.
- You know whether you **already own the Blu-ray** (combo packs often include it).
- For imports, you've checked the **Blu-ray disc's region** in the combo pack.
:::
