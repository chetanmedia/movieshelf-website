**The United States is Blu-ray Region A.** Region A covers all of North, Central and South America plus US territories, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan and parts of Southeast Asia. A US Blu-ray player plays Region A discs and region-free discs, but not discs locked to Region B (Europe, Africa, the Middle East, Australia and New Zealand) or Region C (most of the rest of Asia, including mainland China, India and Russia).

The good news for collectors: 4K Ultra HD Blu-rays have no region coding at all, and plenty of regular Blu-rays are coded for every region. This guide shows you which countries sit in each region, how to check a disc before you buy, and what to do about the imports you really want.

- 3 | Blu-ray regions worldwide | A, B and C
- A | is the US region | shared with Canada, Mexico, Japan and Korea
- 0 | region codes on 4K UHD discs | they play anywhere
:::

<div class="tldr">

**TL;DR**

- The US, Canada and all of Latin America are **Region A**. So are Japan, South Korea and Taiwan.
- The UK and Europe are **Region B**, along with Africa, the Middle East, Australia and New Zealand.
- **Region C** covers the rest of Asia, including mainland China, India and Russia.
- Discs marked **ABC**, or with no badge, are region-free. **4K UHD discs** are always region-free.
- A locked Region B disc won't play on a US player unless the player is region-free.

## Which countries are in Blu-ray Regions A, B and C?

Blu-ray splits the world into three regions, a lot simpler than DVD's eight. Here's the breakdown, based on [Sony's official region guide](https://www.sony.ca/en/electronics/support/articles/00029369) and the [BFI's region notes](https://shop.bfi.org.uk/formats-and-region-codes/):

| Region | Who uses it | Typical import examples |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | United States, Canada, Mexico, Central and South America, US territories, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, parts of Southeast Asia | US Criterion releases, Japanese editions of anime and Ghibli films |
| **B** | UK and Europe, Africa, the Middle East, Australia, New Zealand | UK boutique labels, German mediabooks, Australian releases |
| **C** | Most of the rest of Asia, including mainland China, India, Russia and Central Asia | Indian and Chinese releases |
| **ABC** / region-free | Plays in every region | Many big studio releases |

Two things surprise people. First, **Japan is Region A**, so Japanese Blu-rays usually play fine in the US, even though Japan is Region 2 on DVD. Second, **Mexico shares a region with the US** on Blu-ray, while on DVD it's Region 4.

:::note Blu-ray regions vs DVD regions
DVD uses different numbers entirely: the US and Canada are DVD Region 1, Europe and Japan are Region 2, and so on. A disc being "Region 1" tells you nothing about its Blu-ray region. Track the two separately.
:::

## Why does region coding exist?

Studios release films at different times and through different distributors in each market. Region coding lets a rights holder stop a disc from one market undercutting a release in another. It's enforced by the player: every Blu-ray player has a region set in its firmware, and a locked disc checks that setting before it plays ([Wikipedia: Regional lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_lockout)).

In practice, that means the region rules affect three kinds of collectors:

:::cards
### Import hunters
UK boutique labels, German mediabooks and Australian exclusives are often Region B. These are the discs most likely to give you an error.
### Anime and Japanese film fans
Good news: Japanese Blu-rays are Region A, so they usually play on US players. Subtitles are a different story, so check those.
### Travelers and movers
A player you buy in the UK is Region B. Move to the US with it and your new local discs may not play.
:::

## How do you check a Blu-ray's region before you buy?

Checking takes about ten seconds once you know where to look.

:::steps
### Look at the back of the case
Most Blu-rays print a small region badge on the back cover, usually near the rating and the tech specs. It shows A, B or C. Some discs also print the region on the disc label.
### Read the badge carefully
One letter means the disc is locked to that region. A badge with A, B and C, or no badge at all, usually means region-free. Some discs are coded for two regions, such as A and B.
### Check the listing or label page
For online orders, the product page should state the region. Specialist label stores and import retailers almost always list it in the specs.
### Look up community reports for tricky titles
Collectors on forums and Reddit regularly test imports and post whether they're "actually region-free". A few discs are coded one way but check the player's country setting too, which is worth knowing for expensive imports.
### Record it once you own it
Add the region to your catalog entry so you know before you lend, sell or travel with the disc.
:::

:::warning Combo packs can mix regions
A 4K UHD combo pack may contain a region-free 4K disc plus a Region B Blu-ray. On a US 4K player, the 4K disc plays and the Blu-ray in the same case may not. Check the specs for each disc, not just the box.
:::

## Are 4K Blu-rays region locked?

No. The Ultra HD Blu-ray format has no region coding, so a 4K disc bought in London, Berlin or Tokyo plays on a US 4K player. Sony's support page states that Ultra HD Blu-ray discs are region-free and play on compatible devices wherever they were bought.

That makes 4K the easiest format to import. If a film you want is only out in the UK, buying the 4K edition instead of the Blu-ray often solves the region problem entirely. ## Can a US Blu-ray player play Region B discs?

Not out of the box. A standard US player is set to Region A in its firmware, and most standalone players don't let you change it. Put in a disc locked to Region B and you'll get an error message instead of a menu.

You have a few options:

| Option | How it works | Good to know |
|---|---|---|
| Buy the region-free or 4K edition | Choose the version that plays anywhere | Easiest fix, nothing to change |
| Wait for a US release | Many UK exclusives get a Region A edition later | Not guaranteed, and sometimes the extras differ |
| Use a region-free player | A modified or multi-region player that plays A, B and C | Costs more than a standard player |
| Computer playback | Playback software stores a region setting you can change | Usually only a limited number of changes |

Game consoles follow the same rules for movies. A US console plays Region A and region-free Blu-rays, and every 4K UHD disc if it has a UHD drive.

:::tip Price the region-free player against your import list
If you only want two or three Region B discs, the 4K editions or a later US release usually cost less than a new player. If you buy UK boutique releases every month, a region-free player pays for itself quickly.
:::

## Which Blu-rays are most likely to be region locked?

There's no master list, but some patterns hold:

- **Big studio releases** are often coded for all regions, because studios use the same disc in many markets.
- **Boutique and independent labels** lock more often, because they license films for one territory only. Many labels sell separate US and UK editions of the same film for exactly this reason.
- **Mediabooks and limited editions** from Germany and other European markets are frequently Region B.
- **Japanese releases** are usually Region A, which makes them easy for US collectors to import.

So the question isn't really "is this film region locked?" It's "is *this edition* region locked?" That's another reason to [catalog by edition, not by film](/blog/how-to-catalog-dvd-blu-ray-collection/). The same applies to [steelbooks](/blog/what-is-a-steelbook/): a UK steelbook and a US steelbook of the same film can have different region codes.

## Before you buy an import

Run through this list before you check out. Tick items off as you go:

:::checklist Import checklist
- Confirm the region for **each disc** in the set, not just the box
- Check the **subtitles and audio** languages on the listing
- See if a **4K edition** exists, since it plays anywhere
- Check whether a **US edition** is announced or already out
- Make sure you don't **already own** this film in another edition
- Note the region in your catalog when it arrives
:::

## Import with confidence

Region codes sound complicated, but for a US collector the rule is simple: Region A and region-free discs play, Region B and C locked discs don't, and 4K discs always do. Check the badge, record the region for each edition you own, and you'll never be surprised by an error screen on movie night.
