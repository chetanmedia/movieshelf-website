You can sell DVDs for money on eBay, Facebook Marketplace, buyback apps like Decluttr, or at local used bookstores and record shops, but how much you get depends far more on *which* discs you have than on where you sell them. Most common mainstream DVDs are worth very little. Out-of-print titles, boutique editions, steelbooks and cult films are the ones that pay.

So the smart move is to sort first, price second and pick a selling channel last. This guide shows you how, and which discs you should think twice about before letting go.

<div class="tldr">

**TL;DR**

1. Know what you own first. Scan or list every disc, including the edition.
2. Sort into three piles: **keep**, **list individually**, **sell as a lot**.
3. Check eBay **sold** listings (not asking prices) for anything that might be valuable.
4. Sell valuable discs on eBay, common ones as a local bundle, and leftovers to a buyback app or used store.
5. Before you sell, keep anything that isn't streaming, has a version only on disc, or you'd regret losing.

</div>

## Are old DVDs actually worth any money?

Some are, most aren't. Major studios pressed hits like *Shrek*, *The Matrix* and *Pirates of the Caribbean* by the millions, and that's why buyback sites offer pennies for them or reject them outright. Supply is huge, and anyone who wants those films has had twenty years to buy a copy.

Value comes from scarcity and demand. These are the kinds of discs that tend to sell well:

:::cards
### Out of print, never upgraded
Films that came out on DVD once and never got a Blu-ray or streaming release. Collectors have no other way to own them.
### Boutique labels
Criterion, Arrow, Shout! Factory, Vinegar Syndrome and similar labels, especially limited editions with slipcovers, booklets or posters.
### Steelbooks and limited editions
Numbered runs and retailer exclusives often outlive their price tag. See [what makes a steelbook collectible](/blog/what-is-a-steelbook/).
### Cult, horror and anime
Genres with passionate collector bases. Small-label horror and older anime sets are frequent surprises.
### Complete, clean sets
TV box sets and film collections with every disc, insert and outer sleeve present. Missing pieces kill the price.
### Sealed copies
Still in the original shrink-wrap. Collectors pay a premium for untouched packaging.
:::

:::pullquote
Where you sell matters less than what you're selling. Sort first, price second, list last.
:::

## How do you sort a DVD collection before selling?

Before you list anything, get a complete list of what you own. Selling from memory is how a sealed boutique release ends up in a $20 bundle. A catalog also gives you exact editions, which is what buyers search for.

:::steps
### Catalog everything, edition by edition
Scan each barcode or type each title, and record the format, edition, region and packaging (slipcover, booklet, inserts). The barcode identifies the exact release, not just the film.
### Check condition as you go
Open each case. Make sure the disc is the right one, look for scratches, and check older discs for [disc rot](/blog/disc-rot/). A case with the wrong disc or a damaged one sells for close to nothing.
### Make the three piles
**Keep** for anything you'd miss, **list** for anything sold copies show is worth listing on its own, and **lot** for everything else.
### Price the "list" pile from sold prices
Search eBay for the exact edition and switch on the *Sold items* filter. Asking prices are wishes, but sold prices are the market.
### Decide where each pile goes
Use the table in the next section to match each pile to the place that pays best for it.
:::

You don't need anything fancy for this. Even if you plan to sell most of it, a full list keeps you from underpricing the handful of discs that matter. It also gives you a record of what you sold.

## Where is the best place to sell DVDs for money?

Every channel trades money for effort. Here's how the main options compare:

| Where to sell | Best for | What you get | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| eBay | Rare, out-of-print and collectible editions | Highest prices, minus fees and shipping | High: photos, listings, shipping |
| Facebook Marketplace | Bundles of common discs to local buyers | Cash, no selling fee on local pickup | Medium: messages, meetups |
| Used bookstores and record shops | Quick clearance of mixed piles | Cash or (usually higher) store credit | Low: one trip |
| Buyback apps (Decluttr and similar) | Common discs you just want gone | Low fixed offers, free shipping label | Low: scan, box, ship |
| Yard sale or flea market | Big piles, zero shipping | Small amounts per disc, in cash | Medium: one day of selling |
| Pawn shops | Last resort | Lowest offers | Low |

### eBay: best for valuable discs

eBay is where collectors search for specific editions, so it's where a rare disc gets its real price. The trade-off is cost: eBay's [final value fee](https://www.ebay.com/help/selling/fees-credits-invoices/selling-fees?id=4822) for most Movies & TV sales is 15.3% of the total, plus a per-order fee of $0.30 to $0.40. You also have to photograph, list and ship each disc. That is worth it for a $25 out-of-print box set and not for a $2 DVD.

### Facebook Marketplace: best for bundles

Local buyers on Marketplace like bulk lots: "40 horror DVDs" or "Complete Marvel Blu-rays" with one photo of the spines. There's no shipping and no fee for local pickup, and the discs leave in one go. Meet somewhere public and take cash or an instant payment app.

### Local stores: fastest cash

Used bookstores such as Half Price Books, independent record shops and media exchange stores buy discs over the counter. Call ahead, because buying policies vary by store. Store credit is usually worth more than the cash offer, which helps if you're trading toward upgrades.

### Buyback apps: easiest, lowest payout

Buyback apps let you scan a barcode, get an instant offer and print a free shipping label. They're convenient for a box of common titles, but offers are wholesale-level and many mainstream DVDs get rejected. Run your "lot" pile through one or two of them after you've pulled out everything valuable.

:::versus Sell individually | Sell as a lot
- Best for discs worth more than fees plus shipping
- Gets full collector price for each edition
- One listing, photo set and package per disc
- Slower: some discs take weeks to sell
---
- Best for common titles worth a dollar or two
- One listing moves dozens of discs
- Buyers expect a bulk discount
- Fast: often gone in a day or two locally
:::

## How do you ship DVDs cheaply?

In the US, prerecorded DVDs and Blu-rays qualify for [USPS Media Mail](https://www.usps.com/ship/mail-shipping-services.htm), which is usually the cheapest option for discs. It's slower than Ground Advantage, and packages can be opened for inspection, so keep it to media only. No notes, posters or other merchandise in the box.

:::tip Pack it like a collector would
Wrap the case in bubble wrap or a padded mailer and put slipcovers in a plastic sleeve. A crushed slipcover on a boutique release can turn a happy buyer into a return request.
:::

## Which DVDs should you keep instead of selling?

Selling the bulk of a collection is often the right call, especially if you've upgraded to Blu-ray or 4K. But some discs are cheap to keep and impossible to replace. Before anything goes in a box, check it against this list:

:::checklist Keep it if...
- The film isn't on any streaming service, or rotates on and off them.
- The disc has a version you can't get elsewhere, like the original theatrical cut.
- It has commentaries or documentaries that didn't carry over to newer releases.
- It's out of print and your Blu-ray "upgrade" hasn't been released yet.
- It's a region or import edition you went out of your way to find.
- It has sentimental value, like a gift or the first disc you ever bought.
:::

A classic example is the 2006 *Star Wars* trilogy DVD release, which included the original theatrical cuts as bonus discs. Those versions have never been released on Blu-ray, so collectors still hold on to them. Older DVDs also sometimes carry commentaries or making-of features that later editions dropped. Look at the special features list before you sell.

Your shelf after a good cull might look like this: the rare and the irreplaceable stay, and the duplicates of your 4K upgrades go.

:::shelf After the cull: keepers stay, upgraded duplicates go to the lot pile

- *The Matrix | dvd
- Brazil (Criterion) | dvd

- Jurassic Park | uhd

:::

The highlighted discs are duplicates you've already replaced in 4K. Sell those without a second thought.

:::warning Imports and region codes
If you sell an import, put the region in the listing title. A Region 2 DVD or Region B Blu-ray won't play on most US players, and an unclear listing leads to returns. Not sure what you have? Read our [DVD region codes guide](/blog/dvd-region-codes/).
:::

## How do you get the most money when selling DVDs?

A few habits separate a good sale from a disappointing one:

- **Sell the edition, not the film.** "*The Thing* Scream Factory Collector's Edition Blu-ray with slipcover" gets found. "The Thing DVD" doesn't.
- **Photograph everything that's included:** the disc, the inserts and the slipcover. Buyers pay for completeness they can see.
- **Clean the cases.** Swap cracked cases for new ones of the same size, and remove price stickers if they come off cleanly.
- **Group bundles by theme.** "Complete Pixar DVDs" or "80s action Blu-rays" sells faster than a random pile.
- **Time big sales.** Many sellers find that collectibles move better around the holidays and Black Friday, when gift buyers are shopping.
- **Keep a record of what you sold** so you don't buy the same film back in a year.

:::note Don't forget the digital codes
Unredeemed digital codes in Blu-ray and 4K combo packs often say they're not for resale. Read the insert before you list a code separately, and if the code was already used, say so in the listing.
:::

## Start with one shelf

You don't need to sort the whole collection in a weekend. Pick one shelf, catalog it, and make the three piles. Within an hour you'll know whether you're sitting on a few hundred dollars or just a lot pile, and you'll still have a record of everything that's left. When you're ready to track the rest, [scanning is the fastest way in](/#scan).
