If you're searching for a Letterboxd alternative because you collect DVDs, Blu-rays or 4K discs, the honest answer is that you probably need a different *kind* of app, not a replacement. Letterboxd is a film diary: it records what you watched, when, and what you thought. A collection app is an inventory: it records which discs you own, which edition, and where each one sits on the shelf. Collectors who try to make one do the other's job end up frustrated, and most settle on using both.

This guide explains what each tool is good at, where the popular "Owned list" workaround breaks down, and how to set up a two-app system that takes minutes a week.

**TL;DR**

- **Letterboxd answers "what have I seen?"** Diary, ratings, reviews, watchlist, lists and friends.
- **A collection app answers "what do I own, and where is it?"** Barcodes, formats, editions, box sets and shelf locations.
- **An "Owned" list on Letterboxd works for a few dozen films**, then breaks on duplicates, editions and box sets.
- **The best setup for most collectors is both:** Letterboxd for viewing, a collection app as the inventory of the shelf.

## What is Letterboxd actually built for?

[Letterboxd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letterboxd) is a social network for film lovers, founded in New Zealand in 2011. Its core loop is simple: you watch a film, log it with a date, give it a star rating, and optionally write a review that your followers can read. On top of that sit a watchlist, user-made lists, and [paid Pro and Patron tiers](https://letterboxd.com/about/pro/) that add things like personalized yearly stats.

It's very good at that loop. What it doesn't model is the physical object. Letterboxd knows *Alien* (1979) as a film. It has no idea whether you own it on DVD, on the 2019 4K UHD, or not at all.

:::cards
### Letterboxd is great for
Logging every viewing, rating and reviewing, discovering films through friends, and building ranked lists to share.
### A collection app is great for
Knowing exactly which discs you own, which edition each is, what's in every box set and which shelf it lives on.
### Where they overlap
Both have a watchlist, both can show stats, and both let you search. The difference is what the list is made of.
:::

## Why do collectors look for a Letterboxd alternative?

Read any thread about it on forums like r/dvdcollection and the same three problems come up. People log their viewing on Letterboxd faithfully, then stand in front of their own shelves and still can't remember what they own. They buy a second copy of a film because the diary said "watched," not "owned." And they lose track of discs they've lent to friends, because nothing in a diary records that.

None of that is a flaw in Letterboxd. It's a sign that the question changed from "what did I watch?" to "what's on my shelf?"

:::pullquote
A diary remembers what you watched. An inventory remembers what you own. Collectors need both memories.
:::

## Can you track a physical collection on Letterboxd?

Sort of. The usual workaround is a list called "Owned," "My Blu-ray collection" or "Physical media," and plenty of public examples exist on Letterboxd. It's free, it's in an app you already use, and for a small collection it does the job.

The trouble starts once the collection grows:

:::versus Letterboxd "Owned" list | Collection app
- One entry per **film**, so a DVD and a 4K of the same film collapse into one row
- No barcode scanning: every title is searched and added by hand
- Format and edition live in free-text notes, if anywhere
- Box sets are just several separate films
- No shelf location, so the list can't tell you where a disc is
---
- One entry per **edition**: your DVD and your 4K steelbook are separate items
- Scan the barcode and the release fills in
- Format (DVD, Blu-ray, 4K UHD) is a proper field you can filter on
- Box sets stored as one package with every film inside
- Shelf location on every entry, so search answers "where is it?"
:::

That first point is the big one. Collectors upgrade constantly: DVD to Blu-ray, Blu-ray to 4K, standard case to boutique edition. A film-level list can't show that you own *The Thing* twice in different formats, and it can't warn you before you buy a third copy of the same one. For more on why edition-level records matter, see our guide on [how to catalog a DVD and Blu-ray collection](/blog/how-to-catalog-dvd-blu-ray-collection/).

## Letterboxd vs a collection app: feature by feature

Here's how the two kinds of tool compare on the jobs collectors actually care about:

| Job | Letterboxd | Collection app (e.g. Movieshelf) |
|---|---|---|
| Log a viewing with date and rating | Yes, its core feature | Not the focus |
| Reviews and social feed | Yes | No |
| Watchlist | Yes, of any film | Yes, built from what you own |
| Add by scanning a barcode | No | Yes |
| Track format and edition | Only in notes | Yes, as fields |
| Box sets | Separate films | One package, films inside |
| Where a disc lives on the shelf | No | Yes, shelf location |
| Custom fields (region, price, slipcover) | No | Yes |
| Stats | Viewing stats (Pro tiers) | Collection stats |

The pattern is clear: Letterboxd is organized around *viewings*, a collection app around *objects*. If you want an app that logs viewing and also covers TV shows, look at diary-style trackers such as Trakt instead. If your problem is the shelf, you want an inventory.

## How do you use Letterboxd and a collection app together?

You don't have to choose. Most collectors who are happy with their setup split the jobs cleanly and let each app do what it's good at:

:::steps
### Keep Letterboxd for watching
Log every film you watch, disc or streaming, with your rating and review. Nothing changes here.
### Move "what I own" into a collection app
Scan your shelves in batches. In Movieshelf you can queue up to 10 barcodes, check the matches and import them together.
### Record the collector details once
Format, edition, box set, region and shelf location. You only do this when a disc comes into the house.
### Pick tonight's film from the shelf
Use the collection app's watchlist to choose from discs you already own, then log the viewing on Letterboxd afterwards.
### Retire the "Owned" list
Once the inventory is in the collection app, delete or freeze the old list so you're not keeping two lists in sync.
:::

## Is a collection app a full Letterboxd replacement?

No, and it's worth being clear about that. A collection app won't give you a viewing diary, reviews, or a feed of what your friends watched last night. If those are why you use Letterboxd, keep using it.

What a collection app replaces is the *workaround*: the "Owned" list, the notes app with titles typed in, the photo of your shelf you scroll through in a store. Those are attempts to make a diary or a camera roll act like an inventory, and they get worse the bigger your shelf gets.
