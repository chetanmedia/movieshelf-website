## How do you plan a Halloween movie night from your own collection?

Most Halloween movie night guides are about snacks, cobwebs and which streaming service has what this week. Those are fine, but the hard part is choosing the films, and a collector has a head start: the menu is already on the shelf. Here's a plan that takes about ten minutes.

:::steps
### Pull every horror and Halloween title into one list
Search your catalog by genre, or walk the shelves and note anything spooky. Box sets count: a monster collection might hide five films.
### Pick a theme from the 31 below
A theme does the choosing for you. "Films set on Halloween night" instantly narrows hundreds of discs to a handful.
### Set a runtime budget
Add up the minutes. Two features fit a weeknight; three fit a Saturday. Leave 10–15 minutes between films for refills.
### Order the films light to dark
Fun or family-friendly first, a classic in the middle, the one that actually scares people last.
### Stage the discs and test the player
Stack the cases in viewing order, check for scratches, and confirm a 4K disc actually plays on the player you're using tonight.
:::

If you haven't cataloged your collection yet, this is the moment it pays off: search beats squinting at spines in a dark room. Our guide to [cataloging a DVD and Blu-ray collection](/blog/how-to-catalog-dvd-blu-ray-collection/) shows how to do it shelf by shelf.

## Which Halloween movies work for a family movie night?

Start early, keep it to two films, and preview anything you're unsure about. These pairings are PG or PG-13, but "spooky" means very different things at six and at twelve.

| # | Theme | Double feature | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Witchy sisters | *Hocus Pocus* (1993) + *Practical Magic* (1998) | Two witchy autumn favorites; the second skews teen |
| 2 | Stop-motion night | *The Nightmare Before Christmas* (1993) + *Coraline* (2009) | Handmade creepiness; add *ParaNorman* (2012) for a triple |
| 3 | Kids vs monsters | *The Monster Squad* (1987) + *Monster House* (2006) | Kid heroes, real stakes, lots of laughs |
| 4 | The Addams night | *The Addams Family* (1991) + *Addams Family Values* (1993) | Gothic, funny, easy to quote all week |
| 5 | Ghosts and ghouls | *Ghostbusters* (1984) + *Beetlejuice* (1988) | Comedy first, spooky second |
| 6 | Gateway frights | *Gremlins* (1984) + *Goosebumps* (2015) | A first taste of creature chaos for tweens |

:::tip The 30-minute test
For younger kids, watch the first half hour of an unfamiliar pick yourself on a different night. If you'd hesitate at any scene, swap it for something lighter.
:::

## What are the best classic horror movie night ideas?

Classic horror is where many collections are deepest, thanks to studio box sets and boutique restorations. Black-and-white films also make great "lights off, candles on" viewing.

:::cards
### 7. Universal Monsters
*Dracula* (1931), *Frankenstein* (1931) and *The Wolf Man* (1941). All three run short, so a triple feature still ends early.
### 8. Hammer Horror
*The Curse of Frankenstein* (1957) and *Horror of Dracula* (1958): Peter Cushing and Christopher Lee in color, with plenty of red.
### 9. Black-and-white chills
*The Haunting* (1963) and *Night of the Living Dead* (1968): one all suggestion, one all siege.
### 10. Vincent Price night
*House on Haunted Hill* (1959) and *The Abominable Dr. Phibes* (1971). Camp, menace and that voice.
### 11. Seventies dread
*The Exorcist* (1973) and *The Omen* (1976). Not for the early slot.
### 12. John Carpenter
*Halloween* (1978), *The Fog* (1980) and *The Thing* (1982). The ideal three-film run for a horror fan.
:::

## What are good horror movie marathon themes for adults?

A marathon works best with a thread that connects the films, so the second and third picks feel like a continuation instead of a reset.

| # | Theme | The lineup |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | Set on Halloween night | *Hocus Pocus* → *Trick 'r Treat* (2007) → *Halloween* (1978) |
| 14 | Wes Craven | *A Nightmare on Elm Street* (1984) + *Scream* (1996) |
| 15 | Original vs remake | *The Fly* (1958) + *The Fly* (1986) |
| 16 | Folk horror | *The Wicker Man* (1973) + *Midsommar* (2019) |
| 17 | Haunted houses | *The Changeling* (1980) + *The Conjuring* (2013) |
| 18 | Horror comedy | *Shaun of the Dead* (2004) + *What We Do in the Shadows* (2014) + *Tucker & Dale vs. Evil* (2010) |
| 19 | Possessed objects | *Christine* (1983) + *Ring* (1998) |
| 20 | Creatures in the dark | *Alien* (1979) + *The Descent* (2005) |
| 21 | Modern slow burn | *The Witch* (2015) + *Hereditary* (2018) |
| 22 | Found footage | *The Blair Witch Project* (1999) + *[REC]* (2007) |

Theme 13 is the easiest crowd-pleaser, because all three films take place on Halloween and the tone climbs steadily. [Trick 'r Treat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trick_%27r_Treat) is an anthology set on Halloween night in a small Ohio town, which makes it a perfect bridge between a family film and a slasher. Here's how the runtime adds up, using [Disney's listed 96 minutes for Hocus Pocus](https://d23.com/a-to-z/hocus-pocus-film/):

:::

## What Halloween movie nights can only collectors do?

These are the themes streaming can't offer, because they depend on the specific discs, editions and extras you own.

:::cards
### 23. 4K showcase
Choose the darkest, best-looking horror discs in your 4K collection. Night scenes are a real test of HDR and black levels.
### 24. Commentary night
Rewatch a favorite with the director's commentary on. It turns a film everyone has seen into a new experience.
### 25. The unwatched pile
Every collector has sealed or never-played horror discs. Tonight they get their turn.
### 26. Label roulette
Pull only horror from one boutique label, like Scream Factory, Arrow Video or Vinegar Syndrome, and pick blind.
### 27. Edition showdown
Play the same scene from the DVD, Blu-ray and 4K of one film back to back. Guests usually pick a winner fast.
### 28. Steelbook night
Only films from the display shelf. A good excuse to finally open that sealed steelbook.
:::

## How do you turn a Halloween movie night into a party?

When guests are involved, let them help choose. It makes the night feel shared, and it ends debates before they start.

:::cards
### 29. Shelf roulette
Write the titles from your Halloween list on slips of paper, put them in a bowl (or a plastic pumpkin) and draw for each slot.
### 30. Bring-a-disc swap
Each guest brings one horror film they love. Vote on the lineup, and write down who brought what so every disc goes home with its owner.
### 31. Extras trivia
Before the party, skim a making-of featurette and write five questions. Ask them between films, with candy as prizes.
:::

:::checklist Halloween movie night prep
- Theme chosen and lineup in viewing order
- Total runtime checked against bedtime (or sunrise)
- Discs inspected for scratches and smudges
- Player tested with each disc, especially 4K
- Subtitles and audio track picked in advance
- Snacks, blankets and a backup comedy ready
- Borrowed discs noted so they come back
:::

:::warning Check before guests arrive
Not every player handles every disc. A 4K UHD disc needs a 4K UHD player or a compatible console, and an imported Region B Blu-ray won't play on most US players. Test anything new before the night.
:::
