The Criterion Collection goes on sale at 50% off in four predictable windows each year: a month-long Barnes & Noble sale in summer, another in November, and two 24-hour flash sales on Criterion.com in spring and fall. The November 2026 Barnes & Noble sale is live now, running November 6 to December 6, 2026, with every in-stock Criterion title half off online and in stores. Outside those windows, it rarely pays to buy Criterion at full price.

Most sale guides stop at the dates. This one also covers what each sale includes, where they differ, and how to plan a haul so you don't come home with a second copy of a film you already own.

## When are the Criterion Collection sales each year?

The pattern has been steady for years. Here's how the last two cycles played out:

| Sale | Where | Recent dates | Length | What's off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spring flash sale | Criterion.com | March 18, 2025 · March 24, 2026 | 24 hours from noon ET | 50% off in-stock discs |
| Summer sale | Barnes & Noble | June 26 to July 26, 2026 | About a month | 50% off Criterion titles |
| Fall flash sale | Criterion.com | October 22, 2025 · October 20, 2026 | 24 hours from noon ET | 50% off in-stock discs, no pre-orders |
| November sale | Barnes & Noble | Nov 7 to Dec 7, 2025 · **Nov 6 to Dec 6, 2026** | About a month | 50% off Criterion titles |

Barnes & Noble has run its Criterion sale twice a year for years, which is why collectors call it the "semi-annual" sale. Criterion's own flash sales are often confirmed only shortly before they start, and the last four landed on a Tuesday or Wednesday.

:::bars Days of 50% off Criterion in 2026, by sale

The takeaway: you have roughly two months of half-price Criterion every year, but only two days of it come directly from Criterion. Plan your big buys around the Barnes & Noble windows and treat the flash sales as a second chance.

:::note Black Friday and Amazon
Amazon isn't an official sale partner, but it has repeatedly discounted Criterion to 50% during the same windows. In June 2026 it ran a 50% off Criterion promotion through the end of July, and for Black Friday 2025 it cut a large selection of Criterion 4K and Blu-ray titles to half price. Coverage varies by title, so compare before you buy.

## Barnes & Noble sale vs Criterion.com flash sale: what's different?

Both take 50% off, but the rules around them are not the same, and that changes what you should buy where.

:::versus Barnes & Noble sale | Criterion.com flash sale
- Runs about a month, twice a year
- Online and in stores, with free store pickup
- Free shipping thresholds of $50 to $60 in recent sales
- Pre-orders releasing during the sale have generally been included
- Stock varies by store, and popular titles sell out online
---
- Runs 24 hours, usually March and October
- Online only, at criterion.com
- Free shipping on orders over $75 in recent sales
- In-stock discs only; the October 2026 sale excluded pre-orders
- Out-of-stock titles stay out of stock until the sale ends
:::

The practical difference is time. Barnes & Noble gives you weeks to browse, compare and pick up in person. A flash sale rewards people who already know exactly what they want and check out quickly, because the most wanted box sets and new 4K releases can go out of stock within hours.

The sale isn't the hard part. Knowing exactly what you already own is.
:::

## How much does a Criterion disc cost on sale?

Criterion sets its own list prices, and most new releases follow a simple pattern. At 50% off, that works out to:

| Format | Typical list price | Sale price at 50% off |
|---|---|---|
| 4K UHD (usually with a Blu-ray in the case) | About $50 | About $25 |
| Standard Blu-ray | About $40 | About $20 |
| Box sets | Varies widely | Half of list |

During the 2025 November sale, for example, 4K editions such as *Barry Lyndon* and *The Princess Bride* were listed at $24.99, while Blu-rays such as *Parasite* and *Sullivan's Travels* were $19.99. Big box sets are where the discount gets dramatic: Amazon's summer 2026 promotion listed the 40-film *CC40* set at $399.98 and Wes Anderson's ten-film 4K collection at $249.98.

:::warning Membership stacking is hit or miss
Barnes & Noble's Premium membership normally adds 10% off. Some buyers have reported it stacking on top of the Criterion sale price, but the summer 2026 sale was widely reported as *not* combining with it. Treat any extra discount as a bonus, not part of the plan.
:::

## How should you plan a Criterion sale haul?

The collectors who get the most out of these sales do the work before the sale opens. Here's a plan that works for either kind of sale:

:::steps
### Start with what you already own
Open your catalog and filter it to Criterion, including spine numbers if you track them. You can't spot a duplicate in a store aisle if you're relying on memory.
### Decide which upgrades are worth it
If you own a film on Criterion Blu-ray, check whether the new 4K actually adds something: a new restoration, Dolby Vision, a different cut. Our [4K vs Blu-ray guide](/blog/4k-blu-ray-vs-blu-ray/) covers what to look for.
### Build a ranked list with a budget
Put titles into three tiers: must-buy, nice-to-have, and only if it's in the store. Add up tier one first so the budget decides, not the checkout page.
### Buy scarce titles first
Box sets, limited packaging and new 4K releases sell out first. Grab them in the opening days of a Barnes & Noble sale, or at the very start of a flash sale.
### Check the release dates
At Barnes & Noble, a title that releases before the sale ends is usually discounted; one that releases after usually isn't. Flash sales may skip pre-orders entirely.
### Add each disc the day it arrives
Scan the barcode before it goes on the shelf, so your catalog is right when the next sale comes around.
:::

A good trick is to keep a custom folder called "Wanted" or "Criterion sale" in your collection app. You'll add to it all year as reviews and announcements come out, and when the sale opens, your list is already sorted.

## What should you buy in the Criterion sale?

There's no single right answer, but the best value tends to fall into a few groups:

:::cards
### Box sets
The bigger the list price, the bigger the dollar saving. Director sets and large collections are the classic sale buy.
### New 4K restorations
Recent releases cost the most at list price. November 2026's slate includes *Harold and Maude*, *Marie Antoinette* and *The Right Stuff* in 4K.
### Titles likely to go out of print
Criterion licenses many films for a limited time. When a license lapses, the disc can disappear and prices on the used market climb.
### Films you already love
The safest buy is a film you know you'll rewatch. Blind buys are fun, but they're where sale regret comes from.
:::

Out-of-print Criterion editions are a real thing: some older DVD box sets now sell for hundreds of dollars, as we covered in [rare DVDs worth money](/blog/rare-dvds-worth-money/). Buying an in-print title at half price is almost always cheaper than chasing it later.

:::tip Criterion discs don't include digital copies
Unlike many studio 4K releases, Criterion discs don't come with a digital code. If that matters to you, factor it in when you compare a Criterion edition with a studio release of the same film.
:::

## Before the next sale: a quick checklist

Tick these off before you open the sale page or walk into the store:

:::checklist Criterion sale prep
- Your Criterion titles are cataloged, with format and edition
- Upgrades are flagged only where the new disc is meaningfully better
- A ranked wish list exists, with a total for the must-buy tier
- Release dates are checked against the sale's end date
- Your shipping total clears the free shipping threshold, or you've chosen store pickup
- You've compared prices on Amazon for anything that's out of stock
:::
