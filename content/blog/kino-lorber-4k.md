Kino Lorber is a New York film distributor and boutique home video label that releases Hollywood catalog films, cult movies and silent classics on Blu-ray and 4K UHD. Its 4K discs, mostly under the KL Studio Classics line, usually come as 4K plus Blu-ray combos with new commentaries, and many recent ones use new Dolby Vision masters. The best way to start is with one 4K of a film you already love, such as *The Silence of the Lambs* or *Face/Off*, bought during one of the label's frequent sales.

Kino can be confusing because its catalog is huge and its logos change from spine to spine. This guide sorts out the lines, the best first purchases and the buying habits that keep a Kino shelf affordable.

## What is Kino Lorber, and why do collectors care about it?

Kino Lorber began as Kino International, founded in 1976 by Bill Pence, then a vice president at Janus Films. In 2009 Kino International merged with Lorber HT Digital, Richard Lorber's company, to form Kino Lorber, with a combined catalog of more than 600 titles at the time, according to its [Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kino_Lorber). Today it also runs theatrical distribution and streaming services, but collectors know it for the discs.

:::cards
### Studio films others skip
Kino licenses catalog titles that the big studios no longer release themselves, from '90s thrillers to '50s noir, so it fills gaps on a lot of shelves.
### Commentaries as standard
New audio commentaries by film historians are a Kino habit. *Snake Eyes* on 4K gets one from Steve Mitchell and Nathaniel Thompson.
### A growing 4K slate
Recent releases include new 4K masters for *Dead Again*, *Mimic* (both cuts, each on its own 4K disc) and *Babe*.
### Silent and world cinema
The older Kino Classics side of the catalog covers silent films and restorations of early cinema that few labels touch.
:::

## What do KL Studio Classics, Kino Cult and Kino Classics mean?

The logo on the spine tells you what kind of release it is. Here's how the main lines differ.

| Line | What it releases | Example titles |
|---|---|---|
| **KL Studio Classics** | Licensed Hollywood catalog films, most of the 4K slate | *The Silence of the Lambs*, *Face/Off*, *Snake Eyes*, *Johnny Handsome* |
| **Kino Cult** | Horror, sci-fi, grindhouse and cult rediscoveries, numbered spines | *Alien Outlaw*, *The Dark Power*, *Underworld* (1985), *The Clown Murders* |
| **Kino Classics** | Silent films, early cinema and archival restorations | Silent-era features and restorations |
| **Kino Lorber (main)** | New foreign, independent and documentary films | Recent festival and arthouse titles |

Kino Cult is the newest line. It launched in October 2023 as a disc imprint for "collector-oriented Blu-ray and 4K UHD releases," debuting with 4K restorations of *Alien Outlaw* and *The Dark Power*, per [High-Def Digest](https://ultrahd.highdefdigest.com/tags/show/kinocult). Its spines are numbered, which makes it the Kino line most likely to tempt completists.

## Which Kino Lorber 4K releases should you buy first?

There's no single "best" Kino 4K, but some titles make an easy entry point because the films are well known and the discs were widely reviewed. Start with a film you'd rewatch anyway.

:::cards
### The Silence of the Lambs
One of KL Studio Classics' early 4K UHD releases, from 2021. A good test of how Kino handles a prestige catalog film.
### Face/Off
Sold as a 4K UHD and Blu-ray combo in 2024, and part of Barnes & Noble's 2025 Kino 4K sale.
### Snake Eyes
Reviewed in January 2025, it has a new HDR/Dolby Vision master from a 16-bit 4K scan of the original camera negative, plus a historians' commentary.
### Comedy shelf
*Kingpin*, *Black Sheep*, *Wayne's World 2*, *Uncle Buck* and *Sneakers* have all been released by Kino on 4K.
### Something stranger
For cult fans, start with *Underworld* (1985), Kino Cult's first 4K UHD release, then follow the spine numbers.
### A 2026 title
*Dead Again* (new 4K transfer, two commentaries) and *Mimic* (both cuts on 4K) were among 2026's notable Kino releases.
:::

:::pullquote
Buy the Kino 4K of a film you already love, and let the commentary track teach you why you love it.
:::

Kino's quality depends on the elements each studio provides, so it varies more than labels that restore every film in house. Before paying full price, check a dedicated disc review site for the specific title.

## Are Kino Lorber 4K discs region free?

The 4K UHD disc itself plays in any 4K player, because UHD Blu-ray has effectively no region coding. The catch is the Blu-ray in the combo pack: Kino is a North American label, and its Blu-rays are typically coded for Region A.

:::warning Outside North America?
If your player is Region B (UK, Europe, Australia), buy the 4K edition and use the UHD disc, or check each listing's region line before ordering a Blu-ray-only release. Our guide to [Blu-ray regions A, B and C](/blog/what-blu-ray-region-is-the-us/) explains how the codes work.
:::

## Kino 4K combo or Blu-ray only: which should you buy?

Most Kino 4K releases bundle a Blu-ray, so the real question is whether the 4K upgrade is worth the extra money for that title. If you're new to the format, our explainer on [what 4K UHD actually adds](/blog/what-is-4k-uhd/) covers HDR, resolution and players.

:::versus 4K UHD + Blu-ray combo | Blu-ray only
- New 4K master, often with Dolby Vision or HDR10
- Region-free 4K disc plus a Blu-ray
- Higher price, but deeper sale discounts
- Best for films with strong photography
---
- Cheaper, often well under 4K prices on sale
- Usually Region A
- Plays on any Blu-ray player
- Fine for older, softer or talky films
:::

:::tip Log the edition, not just the film
Kino has released some films on Blu-ray first and on 4K years later. Record the format, the line (KL Studio Classics, Kino Cult) and whether it came with a slipcover, so you can tell an upgrade from a duplicate at a sale.
:::

## When does Kino Lorber have sales, and where should you buy?

Kino discounts often, which is the main reason to never pay full price. In 2026 its own store, kinolorber.com, ran named promotions including an Oscars sale, a Cannes sale and a Fall Haul sale. Retailers run their own: from May 15 to June 15, 2025, Barnes & Noble had Kino 4K titles at 50% off, alongside its regular Criterion sales.

:::steps
### Build a wishlist before the sale
Write down the exact edition you want: 4K combo or Blu-ray, and which cut if there are several, like *Mimic*.
### Check what you already own
Kino's catalog overlaps with older studio releases. Search your catalog before checkout so you don't rebuy a film you have on another label.
### Compare the store and retailers
Kino's own store sales covered 4K UHDs from $14.99 in spring 2025. Retailer promotions sometimes beat that on newer titles.
### Watch the shipping threshold
In that 2025 sale, kinolorber.com offered free shipping on orders of $60 or more, so batch your order.
### Catalog the haul on delivery
Scan the discs the day they arrive, while the receipt and edition details are fresh.
:::

## How big has the Kino Lorber catalog grown?

The numbers explain why a Kino wishlist gets long fast. The combined catalog at the 2009 merger was a fraction of what the label lists today.

:::bars Kino Lorber library size
- At the 2009 merger | 600 | 600+ titles
- Library today | 4000 | 4,000+ titles
:::

Not all of those are on disc, and many are DVD-era releases, but the scale means you'll keep finding Kino spines in thrift stores and used bins.

## How do you keep a Kino Lorber collection organized?

A label this large rewards a few simple habits:

:::checklist Kino Lorber collector routine
- Record the **line** for every disc: KL Studio Classics, Kino Cult, Kino Classics
- Note **Kino Cult spine numbers** if you're collecting the line
- Mark **4K combo vs Blu-ray only**, and which cut is included
- Note the **region** on every Blu-ray if you play imports
- Keep a **wishlist** of exact editions before each sale
- Add new arrivals **the day they're delivered**
:::
