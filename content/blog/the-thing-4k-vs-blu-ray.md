**For John Carpenter's *The Thing* (1982), the 4K UHD is the version to watch.** Universal's 2021 4K disc presents the film in native 4K from the original camera negative, with HDR10 and a new DTS:X soundtrack, and it clearly beats every Blu-ray on picture and sound. The catch is extras: the 4K carries only a handful of legacy features, so the Scream Factory Collector's Edition Blu-ray is still worth keeping next to it.

That's the short answer. The longer one depends on which Blu-ray you already own, because *The Thing* has had four very different HD releases, and the disc bundled in the 4K box is not the one you might expect.

:::stats
- 4× | the pixels of a 1080p Blu-ray | 3840×2160 vs 1920×1080
- 2021 | Universal's 4K UHD release | September 7 in the US
- 4 | HD transfers since 2008 | 2008, 2016, 2017 and 2021
:::

<div class="tldr">

**TL;DR**

- **Best picture and sound:** Universal's 2021 4K UHD (HDR10, DTS:X, native 4K from the camera negative).
- **Best extras:** Scream Factory's 2016 Collector's Edition Blu-ray. Keep it if you have it.
- **Skip as an upgrade:** the 2008 Universal Blu-ray. It's also the disc inside the 4K combo pack.
- **Imports:** Arrow's 2017 UK Blu-ray has a Carpenter-approved 4K-scan restoration, but it's Region B only.

## What are the differences between The Thing on 4K and Blu-ray?

Here's every major HD edition side by side. The specs come from the discs' published listings and from reviews such as [The Digital Bits' 4K review](https://mail.digitalbits.com/reviews/item/thing-1982-universal-4k-2021).

| Edition | Source | HDR | Main audio | Extras | Region |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Universal 4K UHD (2021) | Original camera negative, native 4K | HDR10 | DTS:X | Commentary, *Terror Takes Shape*, outtakes, trailer | Free |
| Scream Factory Collector's Edition Blu-ray (2016) | New 2K scan of the interpositive, approved by Dean Cundey | — | DTS-HD MA 5.1, plus a 4.1 mix from the 70mm six-track | Two discs of new and legacy extras | A |
| Arrow Video Blu-ray, UK (2017) | 4K scan of the original negative, supervised by Carpenter and Cundey | — | DTS-HD MA 5.1 | New documentaries plus legacy extras | B |
| Universal Blu-ray (2008) | Older HD master | — | DTS-HD MA 5.1 | Legacy extras via U-Control | A |

The headline difference is the source. The 2008 Blu-ray came from an older master that reviewers found soft and smoothed over, with a warm, pinkish cast to skin tones. The 4K goes back to the camera negative, and the extra resolution and HDR show up most in the things you watch *The Thing* for: Rob Bottin's creature effects, the Antarctic exteriors and the dark interiors of Outpost 31.

:::

## How much better does The Thing look in 4K?

On resolution alone, a 4K disc holds four times the pixels of a Blu-ray. That only matters if the source has the detail to fill them, and a 35mm camera negative does.

:::bars Pixels per frame (millions)
- 4K UHD (3840×2160) | 8.29 | 8.3 million
- Blu-ray (1920×1080) | 2.07 | 2.1 million
:::

Reviewers agree the upgrade is real. The Digital Bits called the 4K the best the film has looked on home video, with light-to-medium, organic grain and no sign of heavy noise reduction. Blu-ray.com rated it a significant step up from the 2008 Blu-ray and "highly recommended." The HDR10 grade adds richer color, deeper shadows and brighter highlights rather than a flashy new look, which suits a film that lives in dim corridors and white-out snow.

:::note No Dolby Vision
Every US pressing of the 4K, including the Best Buy steelbook and the 2026 Walmart exclusive, is **HDR10 only**. If your TV supports Dolby Vision, you won't see it on this disc.
:::

## Is the Blu-ray in the 4K combo pack the new restoration?

No, and this trips a lot of buyers up. The 1080p disc inside Universal's 4K UHD set is **the same Blu-ray Universal released in 2008**, complete with the old U-Control picture-in-picture features. Only the 4K disc carries the new restoration.

That matters in two situations:

- **You only own a Blu-ray player.** Buying the 4K set gets you the old 2008 transfer. Scream Factory's Collector's Edition is the better Blu-ray to buy.
- **You were planning to sell your old Blu-ray.** If it's the 2008 Universal disc, you now have it twice. If it's the Scream Factory set, think twice before selling it (see below).

:::warning Check the disc, not just the box
The 4K box and the 2008 Blu-ray carry the same studio branding. If you catalog editions, record the 4K set and the standalone 2008 Blu-ray as separate entries so you know which discs you actually have.
:::

## Should you keep the Scream Factory Blu-ray?

For most collectors, yes. The 4K wins on picture and sound, but Scream Factory's 2016 Collector's Edition is the definitive *supplemental* package, and almost none of it is on the 4K.

:::cards
### New commentaries
Tracks with cinematographer Dean Cundey and with co-producer Stuart Cohen, alongside the classic Carpenter and Kurt Russell commentary.
### Hours of interviews
*Requiem for a Shape Shifter* (Carpenter in conversation with Mick Garris), *The Men of Outpost 31* and pieces on the effects, editing and music.
### A 70mm-style mix
A 4.1 DTS-HD track built from the original 70mm six-track Dolby Stereo soundtrack, for purists who want the 1982 sound.
### The TV version
The network broadcast cut in standard definition, a curiosity that isn't on any other current disc.
:::

The 4K, by contrast, includes the Carpenter and Russell commentary, the 84-minute documentary *John Carpenter's The Thing: Terror Takes Shape*, outtakes and the trailer. Those are good, but they're the same extras that have been around since the DVD days.

:::pullquote
Watch the 4K, keep the Scream Factory set: one disc for the picture, the other for the stories behind it.
:::

## Is Arrow's UK Blu-ray worth importing?

Arrow Video's 2017 UK release has the most interesting pedigree of any Blu-ray: a [restoration from a 4K scan of the original negative](https://www.arrowfilms.com/p/the-thing-limited-edition-blu-ray/11515183/), supervised and approved by John Carpenter and Dean Cundey. It also came with new feature-length documentaries, *Who Goes There? In Search of The Thing* and *1982: One Amazing Summer*.

It's still only 1080p and standard dynamic range, though, and it's locked to **Region B**, so it won't play on most North American Blu-ray players. For US collectors it's a completist's pickup, not an upgrade over the 4K. If you do import it, a [region-free Blu-ray player](/blog/best-region-free-blu-ray-player/) is the only reliable way to play it.

## Which version of The Thing should you buy?

It comes down to what you already own and what you play discs on:

:::steps
### You own a 4K player and no copy yet
Buy the Universal 4K UHD. Any pressing works: standard, Best Buy steelbook or the 2026 Walmart edition all share the same HDR10 and DTS:X specs.
### You own the 2008 Universal Blu-ray
Upgrade to the 4K. It's a clear improvement in every technical area, and you lose nothing because the 2008 disc comes in the box.
### You own the Scream Factory Collector's Edition
Add the 4K for picture and sound, and keep the Scream Factory set for its extras and the 4.1 mix.
### You only have a Blu-ray player
Buy the Scream Factory Collector's Edition, not the 4K combo pack, which would give you the older 2008 transfer.
### You collect imports and steelbooks
Arrow's 2017 UK limited edition and the UK and Best Buy 4K steelbooks are the collectible ones. Check the region on anything from overseas.
:::

## Which steelbooks and special editions of The Thing exist?

The film has had a lot of variants, which is exactly why collectors end up with duplicates. The main ones you'll see on the secondhand market:

- **Best Buy 4K steelbook (US, 2021):** the 4K and 2008 Blu-ray in a [steelbook](/blog/what-is-a-steelbook/), same specs as the standard 4K.
- **UK 4K editions (2021):** Universal released the 4K in the UK on September 20, 2021, plus a pricier limited collector's edition with fancier packaging, the soundtrack and a bonus disc.
- **Walmart exclusive 4K (US, 2026):** a reissue with a slipcover and the same HDR10 and DTS:X specs.
- **Arrow limited edition and steelbook (UK, 2017):** Blu-ray only, Region B.

Before you buy another one, scan the barcode or check your list. Each of these editions has its own barcode, so a catalog that tracks editions will tell you exactly which version is already on your shelf.

:::checklist Before you buy another copy of The Thing
- Check which Blu-ray you own: 2008 Universal, Scream Factory or Arrow.
- Confirm your player handles 4K UHD, not just upscaling.
- Decide whether you're buying for picture, extras or packaging.
- Check the region on any import before ordering.
- Add the new edition to your catalog the day it arrives.
:::
