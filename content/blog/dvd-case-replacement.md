**For a DVD case replacement, buy a standard 14 mm DVD keep case (about 190 x 135 mm), or for Blu-ray and 4K UHD a case about 171.5 x 136 mm and 11 to 13 mm thick, with the same disc count and spine thickness as the one that cracked.** Match the color too: clear or black for DVD, blue for Blu-ray, black for 4K. Get those four things right and your cover art, slipcover and shelf line all fit like the original.

Cases crack, hubs snap, and plenty of modern releases ship in cut-away "eco" cases that collectors swap out on day one. This guide gives you the real sizes in millimeters and inches, explains which details actually matter when you buy a replacement, and covers the cases that need special care.

:::stats
- 190 mm | height of a standard DVD case | about 7.5 in
- 171.5 mm | height of a Blu-ray or 4K UHD case | about 6.75 in
- $3–5 | typical price of one boutique-label spare case | Vinegar Syndrome, Severin
:::

## What size are DVD, Blu-ray and 4K UHD cases?

These are the dimensions published by packaging suppliers. Individual makers vary by a millimeter or so, which is why you should always measure the case you're replacing.

| Case type | Height | Width | Thickness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard DVD keep case | 190 mm (7.5 in) | 135 mm (5.3 in) | 14 mm (0.55 in) | Often called an Amaray-style case |
| Slim DVD case | 190 mm (7.5 in) | 135 mm (5.3 in) | 7–8 mm (0.3 in) | Common in TV season box sets |
| Standard Blu-ray case | 171.5 mm (6.75 in) | 135–136 mm (5.3 in) | 11–13 mm (0.5 in) | Translucent blue, Blu-ray banner on top |
| 4K UHD case | 171.5 mm (6.75 in) | 136 mm (5.35 in) | 12–12.5 mm (0.5 in) | Black, 4K Ultra HD banner on top |
| Multi-disc Blu-ray / 4K | 171.5 mm (6.75 in) | 136 mm (5.35 in) | 12.5–15 mm | Hinged trays or stacked hubs inside |

Sources: [CDROM2GO's Blu-ray and DVD case specs](https://cdrom2go.com/blog/whats-the-difference-between-blu-ray-cases-and-dvd-cases), [Duplication.com's 4K UHD case listings](https://www.duplication.com/4k-uhd-cases) and [Severin's replacement case page](https://severinfilms.com/shop/black-blu-ray-cases).

:::bars Case height, standard sizes (mm)
- DVD case | 190 | 190 mm
- Blu-ray case | 171.5 | 171.5 mm
- 4K UHD case | 171.5 | 171.5 mm
:::

The headline difference is height. A Blu-ray case is about 18.5 mm (three-quarters of an inch) shorter than a DVD case, which is why a DVD dropped into a Blu-ray shelf sticks up so obviously. Width is essentially the same, and Blu-ray cases are usually a millimeter or two thinner.

### What size is the cover art insert?

The paper wrap under the clear sleeve is what you'll be moving into the new case, so it matters most.

- **DVD insert:** 273 x 183 mm flat (about 10.75 x 7.2 in), including a 14 mm spine. [Avery sells printable blanks](https://www.avery.co.uk/product/dvd-case-inserts-j8437-25) at exactly this size.
- **Blu-ray insert:** about 148 mm (5.8 in) tall and roughly 270 mm wide flat, depending on the spine. It's shorter than the case because the logo banner takes up the top of the sleeve.

:::note Why the banner matters
Blu-ray and 4K art is printed to sit *under* a molded logo banner. Put it in a bannerless clear case and you'll see a gap above the artwork. Put it in a DVD case and it floats in the middle of the sleeve.
:::

## Which details matter when you buy a replacement case?

Dimensions get you close. These four details decide whether the replacement looks like it came from the studio.

:::cards
### Spine thickness
A 14 mm insert wrapped around a 12 mm case leaves a soft fold on the spine, and a thick case won't slide back into a slipcover. Match within a millimeter.
### Disc count
Single, double, triple and quad cases share a footprint but not a thickness. A 4-disc 4K case can be 15 mm, so count the discs first.
### Color and banner
Blue Blu-ray, black 4K, clear for many boutique labels. On a shelf sorted by format, one wrong color stands out instantly.
### Hub style
Push-button hubs release the disc when you press the center. Some collectors prefer firmer "pluck" hubs because discs can pop loose from weak push-buttons in transit.
:::

:::pullquote
Measure the spine before you order. It's the one detail that decides whether the slipcover still fits.
:::

Collectors on the DVD Talk forum have [compared notes on replacement hubs](https://forum.dvdtalk.com/dvd-talk-covers-cases-inserts/575371-where-find-good-replacement-cases-type-hubs-most-secure.html) for years, and the consistent complaint is cheap bulk cases with flimsy hubs that arrive broken or let discs rattle. Paying a little more per case for a known brand is usually worth it.

## How do you replace a cracked DVD or Blu-ray case?

The swap takes about five minutes. The care is in not damaging the artwork or the disc on the way.

:::steps
### Measure the old case
Check height, spine thickness and disc count with a ruler. Note the color and whether there's a logo banner.
### Order a matching case
Buy from the label that released the disc if it sells spares, or a packaging supplier that lists exact millimeter sizes.
### Lift the disc straight off the hub
Press the center button and lift by the edges. Never bend the disc to pop it free; that's how hairline cracks start near the center hole.
### Slide the insert out slowly
Ease the wrap out from the open edge of the sleeve. If it's stuck from static or age, a thin, dull plastic card helps more than fingers.
### Move everything else across
Booklets, reversible art, digital-code slips, and any clips or inner artwork tray. Check the old case for a second disc hiding under a hinged tray.
### Close it inside the slipcover
Before you recycle the old case, test the new one inside the slipcover. If it binds, the case is too thick.
:::

:::warning Printed inner artwork stays behind
Some releases print artwork on the inside of the case itself, not on a separate sheet. That can't be moved. For limited editions and boutique releases where the original case matters to resale, keep the cracked case alongside the disc.
:::

## What about eco cases, slipcovers and steelbooks?

Not every case is a standard keep case, and the edge cases are where people buy the wrong thing.

:::versus Swap the case | Keep the original
- Eco cases with cut-out holes that flex and crack
- Cracked hinges and snapped hubs on standard releases
- DVD-height cases on Blu-rays from older reissues
---
- Steelbooks, which aren't replaceable with plastic
- Cases with printed inside art on limited editions
- Sealed copies you might sell
:::

- **Eco cases.** Many studio Blu-rays and 4K discs ship in lighter cases with holes cut in the plastic. They work, but they flex and the sleeve tears easily. A standard case of the same thickness is a straight swap.
- **Slipcovers.** The slipcover was sized around the original case. Same-thickness replacements slide in cleanly; anything thicker crushes the slip's edges over time.
- **Steelbooks.** A dented steelbook can't be "re-cased." If the disc tray inside cracks, some collectors move the disc into a plain case and store the steel separately. Our guide to [what a steelbook is](/blog/what-is-a-steelbook/) covers how to protect them.
- **Box sets.** Digipaks and fold-out trays are specific to each release. Replacement trays rarely match, so a cracked digipak is usually a "live with it or find another copy" situation.

## Where do you buy replacement cases?

You have three realistic sources, each suited to a different job.

| Source | Good for | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| The label's own store | Boutique releases (clear or black cases with the right logo) | Spine size and disc count before ordering |
| Packaging suppliers | Packs of 10 to 100 standard DVD, Blu-ray or 4K cases | Listed thickness in mm and hub type |
| Marketplaces and big-box stores | One-off replacements in a hurry | Reviews mentioning broken hubs or wrong sizes |

Boutique labels are the easiest win. Vinegar Syndrome sells [clear Blu-ray and black 4K cases](https://vinegarsyndrome.com/products/blu-ray-cases-2-disc-clear-viva-elite) in 1-, 2- and 3-disc sizes, and Severin sells black Blu-ray and 4K cases in single to 4-disc sizes, each for a few dollars. Buying a small pack also covers the next crack.

## How do you keep cases from cracking in the first place?

Most cracked cases come from three things: shelves packed too tight, stacking discs flat under weight, and cold plastic being flexed open. A few habits help:

:::checklist Case care routine
- Leave a finger's width of slack on every shelf so cases slide out without twisting
- Store discs upright, not in tall flat stacks
- Open cases from the edge, not by bending the spine
- Keep a small stock of spare DVD, Blu-ray and 4K cases
- Swap eco cases before they tear the cover art
- Check cases for loose discs when you reorganize a shelf
:::

Good storage protects the discs too. Heat and humidity that warp plastic also speed up the chemical decay described in our [disc rot guide](/blog/disc-rot/), and a shelf system with breathing room is easier to keep tidy. Walk your shelves once, pull every cracked case and eco case you want to retire, and measure them into three piles: DVD, Blu-ray and 4K. One small order covers the lot. While you're there, add a case note to each entry in your catalog so the details stay with the disc, not in your head.
