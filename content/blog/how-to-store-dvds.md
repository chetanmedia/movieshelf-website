**To store DVDs and Blu-rays so they last, keep them upright like books, in cases that hold the disc by its center hub, in a cool, dry, dark room with stable temperature.** Avoid attics, garages, basements and sunny walls. If you need to save space, soft non-woven or felt-lined sleeves work, but cheap plastic wallets and tight sleeves are the most common way collectors damage discs in storage.

The good news is that discs are tough. Most of the damage people find years later comes from a handful of storage mistakes, and every one of them is easy to avoid once you know what they are.

:::stats
- 20–100 years | estimated life of pressed discs like DVDs | NIST care guide
- Below 68°F | recommended storage temperature | 20–50% relative humidity
- 35–40% | humidity target for home collections | Library of Congress
:::

<div class="tldr">

**TL;DR**

1. Store discs **upright**, never in tall flat stacks.
2. Keep them in a **cool, dry, dark room** with steady temperature. No attic, garage or basement floor.
3. Use cases that **grip the center hub**, so nothing touches the data surface.
4. Going caseless? Use **soft, loose sleeves**, not cheap PVC wallets.
5. Packing for a move or storage unit: **lidded boxes, upright, climate-controlled**, and a list of what's in each box.

</div>

## What is the best way to store DVDs and Blu-rays?

Every serious guide to disc care, from the [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/preservation/care/record.html) to the [NIST guide for librarians and archivists](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/NISTspecialpublication500-252.pdf), comes back to the same four rules. They apply equally to DVDs, Blu-rays and 4K UHD discs.

:::cards
### Upright
Stand cases on edge like books. Flat stacks load the bottom cases with weight and make every disc harder to reach.
### Cool and stable
Room temperature or a little below. Steady matters more than exact: big daily swings are worse than a slightly warm room.
### Dry
Humidity speeds up corrosion of the thin reflective layer inside the disc, the main cause of [disc rot](/blog/disc-rot/).
### Dark
Keep shelves out of direct sunlight. UV fades cover art and slipcovers and makes plastic cases brittle long before it bothers the disc.
:::

The Library of Congress puts the home version simply: cool, around 35–40% relative humidity, clean and stable, and away from attics, basements, radiators and vents. You don't need a hygrometer to get there. A shelf on an inside wall of a lived-in room already meets most of it.

:::pullquote
Discs rarely fail on a good shelf. They fail in attics, car trunks and plastic wallets.
:::

## Should DVDs be stored vertically or horizontally?

Vertically. Upright storage is the one point every care guide agrees on, and it's also the easiest to browse, because you can read every spine.

:::versus Upright, like books | Flat stacks
- Weight spread evenly; no case carries another
- Every spine readable at a glance
- Pull one disc without moving ten
- Leaves room to slot in new arrivals
---
- Bottom cases carry the whole stack
- Hubs crack and cases warp under weight
- You unstack half a pile to reach one film
- Stacks topple when you pull from the middle
:::

Leave a finger's width of slack on each shelf. Cases packed in too tight twist when you pull them out, which is how most hubs and spines crack. If you have already lost a few, our guide to [DVD case replacement](/blog/dvd-case-replacement/) covers the exact sizes to buy.

## Which storage option protects discs best?

Original cases win on protection. Everything else trades some protection for space, and that's a reasonable trade for some collections as long as you know what you're giving up.

| Storage option | Protection | Space used | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Original keep case | Best: disc held by hub, nothing touches the surface | Most | Cracked hubs from tight shelves |
| Slim case | Very good | About half a standard case | Thin plastic cracks more easily |
| Felt-lined or non-woven sleeves | Good if loose and clean | Small | Grit trapped in the lining scratches discs |
| Hard plastic sleeves or PVC wallets | Poor | Smallest | Scratches, residue, sticking in heat |
| Steelbook | Very good for the disc | Similar to a standard case | Dents and scuffs on the steel itself |

:::bars Roughly how many cases fit on one foot of shelf
- DVD keep case (~14–15 mm) | 20 | ~20
- Blu-ray or 4K case (~12–13 mm) | 23 | ~23
- Slim DVD case (~7–8 mm) | 40 | ~40
:::

Those figures come from the standard case thicknesses in our [case size guide](/blog/dvd-case-replacement/). Slim cases alone nearly double a shelf's capacity without the disc ever touching anything, which makes them the safest space-saver.

## How do you store DVDs without cases?

Plenty of collectors go caseless to fit a big collection into a small home. It works, but this is where most storage damage happens, so the details matter.

:::steps
### Choose soft, loose sleeves
Felt-lined, non-woven, polypropylene or polyethylene sleeves are the usual choices. Avoid cheap PVC wallets, which [What Hi-Fi? warns](https://www.whathifi.com/tv-home-cinema/blu-ray-players/warning-if-youre-storing-your-dvds-and-blu-rays-like-this-you-could-be-damaging-them) can soften in heat and fuse to a disc.
### Clean each disc before it goes in
Grit trapped in a sleeve becomes sandpaper. Wipe from the center out in straight lines with a microfiber cloth.
### Slide, don't force
If a disc needs pushing, the sleeve is too tight. Every insertion drags the surface across the lining.
### Keep the artwork
Slip the cover insert into the same sleeve if it has a pocket, or file covers in a binder. Covers, booklets and slipcovers carry much of a disc's resale value.
### Store the sleeves upright and cool
A box or binder of sleeves follows the same rules as a shelf of cases: upright, dry, out of the sun, never in a car.
:::

:::warning Test before you sleeve the whole collection
In one r/dvdcollection thread, a collector who moved about 800 DVDs into plastic sleeves found them blotchy a year later and had to clean them one by one, while the Blu-rays in the same sleeves were fine. Put a handful of discs into any new sleeve for a few months before committing hundreds.
:::

If you do go caseless, keep a few empty cases around. A disc you lend out, or one you decide to sell, travels much better in a proper case.

## How do you store steelbooks, box sets and 4K discs?

Most of the same rules apply, with a few format-specific habits:

- **Steelbooks:** the disc is safe on its hub, but the steel dents and scuffs. Store them upright with a little slack, and use clear protector sleeves if you care about display condition. Our [steelbook guide](/blog/what-is-a-steelbook/) covers editions and care.
- **Box sets:** fold-out digipaks often hold discs in cardboard slots. Avoid sliding discs in and out of paper sleeves more than you need to, and store heavy sets upright on a low shelf.
- **4K UHD:** physically, a 4K disc is as tough as any Blu-ray. Treat it the same, and keep any included Blu-ray and digital code leaflet in the same case.

## How do you pack DVDs for long-term storage or a move?

Boxing discs for a move, a storage unit or a spare room is where good collections quietly get ruined. Run through this before you seal the first box:

:::checklist Packing discs for storage
- Use **small, sturdy boxes or lidded plastic bins**. A full box of DVDs is heavy, so don't oversize.
- Pack cases **upright, spine up**, snug enough not to shift but not crushed.
- Fill gaps with paper or bubble wrap so cases can't slide when the box tilts.
- Skip vacuum bags and anything that **presses on the cases**.
- Choose **climate-controlled** storage over a garage, attic or plain outdoor unit.
- Keep boxes **off concrete floors** and away from exterior walls.
- **Number every box** and record what's in it before you tape it shut.
- After a cold journey, let boxes reach **room temperature** before opening and playing discs.
:::

The last two points are the ones people skip. The Library of Congress specifically advises letting discs from cool storage warm to room temperature before playback, so condensation never forms on a cold disc. And a numbered box is only useful if you know what's inside it.

## Where should you never store DVDs?

:::cards
### The attic
The hottest room in summer and often the coldest in winter. That swing is exactly what care guides warn against.
### The garage
Temperature swings, damp, dust and exhaust fumes, all in one place.
### The basement floor
Humidity collects low down, and a single leak can soak a whole box.
### The car
A parked car in summer gets far hotter than any room. Never leave discs or sleeve wallets in one.
### A sunny window wall
UV fades spines and slipcovers and makes plastic brittle.
### On top of the TV or receiver
Electronics vent heat straight up into whatever sits above them.
:::

## Frequently asked questions

:::takeaways
- Store discs **upright, cool, dry and dark**, with steady temperature.
- **Original or slim cases** are the safest storage; slim cases nearly double shelf capacity.
- Caseless? Use **soft, loose sleeves**, clean discs first, and test before converting everything.

## Check your shelf tonight

Walk past your collection and look for three things: a stack lying flat, a shelf packed too tight, and anything in direct sun. Fixing those takes ten minutes, and it's most of what keeps a collection playable for decades.
