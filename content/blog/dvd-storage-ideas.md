**The best DVD storage ideas keep your cases upright, spines out, on shelves no deeper than a case is wide.** For a small collection that means picture ledges, floating shelves or a media console. For a few hundred discs, cube units and closet shelving. For a thousand or more, a wall of shallow bookcases with extra shelves added. If you want them out of sight, drawers, ottomans and under-bed boxes work as long as the discs stand up and you can still find them.

Most DVD storage roundups stop at a pretty photo. This one adds what you actually need before you buy anything: how many discs each idea holds, which ones suit Blu-ray and 4K, and how to find a single film once your collection is behind doors.

:::stats
- 20 | DVDs per foot of shelf | about 23 for Blu-ray or 4K cases
- ~44 | DVDs in one Kallax cube | two rows deep, about 50 Blu-rays
- ~400 | DVDs on one 80 cm Billy bookcase | with extra shelves, single row
:::

**TL;DR**

- **Under 200 discs:** a media console, floating shelves or picture ledges.
- **200 to 800:** Kallax-style cube units, closet shelving or a rotating tower.
- **800+:** shallow bookcases with extra shelves, wall to wall.
- **Hidden:** ottomans, benches, under-bed drawers and door-fronted cubes, discs upright.
- **Whatever you choose:** give every shelf, box or drawer a label and record it, or hidden storage becomes lost storage.

## How much space does a DVD collection actually need?

Start with the numbers, because they decide which ideas are realistic. A standard DVD keep case is about 190 mm tall, 135 mm wide and 14 mm thick. Blu-ray and 4K UHD cases share the same width but are about 171.5 mm tall and 11 to 13 mm thick. Our [DVD case size guide](/blog/dvd-case-replacement/) has the full measurements.

With a little slack between cases, that works out to roughly:

:::bars Discs per foot of shelf (approximate)
- DVD keep case | 20 | ~20
- Blu-ray or 4K case | 23 | ~23
:::

So 300 DVDs need about 15 feet of shelf, and 1,000 need about 50. Two depth rules matter just as much. A case is 13.5 cm wide, so anything deeper than about 15 cm wastes space unless you double-row. And a DVD needs a shelf opening of about 21 cm, a Blu-ray about 19 cm. Furniture with fixed 30 cm gaps loses a third of its height to air.

:::tip Measure before you shop
Count your discs by format, multiply by the per-foot figure above, then add 25% for growth. That single number rules out half the furniture in any store.
:::

## What are the best DVD storage ideas for small collections?

Under about 200 discs, you can treat the collection as part of the room rather than storage.

:::cards
### 1. Media console with drawers
Many TV stands have drawers deep enough for cases standing spine up. Check the inside height is over 19 cm before buying.
### 2. Picture ledges, face out
IKEA's [MOSSLANDA ledge](https://www.ikea.com/ie/en/p/mosslanda-picture-ledge-black-70292104) is 115 cm long and 12 cm deep. About eight covers fit face out per ledge. Great for a rotating "now watching" display.
### 3. Floating shelves at case height
Space them about 21 cm apart for DVDs or 19 cm for Blu-ray. A 90 cm shelf holds roughly 60 DVDs.
### 4. A bookshelf you already own
Add extra shelf pins to split a 30 cm gap into a case-height row. It's the cheapest upgrade on this list.
### 5. A single cube unit
A 2x2 cube unit fits behind a sofa or under a window and holds close to 180 DVDs double-rowed.
:::

## What are the best hidden DVD storage ideas?

Hidden storage keeps a living room calm, but it has one real cost: you can't browse the spines. The ideas below all work, as long as the cases stand upright inside.

:::cards
### 6. Lift-top storage ottoman
Discs stand in rows inside, spines up. A good fit for the 50 films you watch most.
### 7. Storage bench under a window
Same idea at a larger scale. Measure the inside height, since many benches are shallower than they look.
### 8. Door and drawer inserts in cube units
Kallax-style units take door and drawer inserts, so one cube can be on display and the next closed.
### 9. Under-bed rolling drawers
Shallow drawers keep cases spine up, like a filing system. Fine for the overflow you rarely touch.
### 10. Closet shelving
A hall or bedroom closet with case-height shelves can hold hundreds of discs, and stays cooler and darker than a sunny wall.
### 11. Over-the-door rack
Narrow rack shelves on the back of a door use space nothing else can. Make sure the door still closes with the cases on it.
:::

:::versus Open shelves | Hidden storage
- Every spine is visible, so browsing works
- Easy to spot gaps and misfiles
- Collects dust and fades in direct sun
---
- Calm room, no visual clutter
- Protects against dust and light
- You need a catalog to find anything
:::

:::warning Not the attic or garage
Out of sight shouldn't mean out of the house. Attics and garages swing between hot and cold, and the [NIST care guide](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/legacy/sp/NISTspecialpublication500-252.pdf) recommends keeping discs cool, dry and stable. A closet in the main part of the house is far safer. Our guide on [how to store DVDs](/blog/how-to-store-dvds/) covers the details.
:::

## How do you store a large DVD or Blu-ray collection?

Past 800 discs, furniture choice becomes a capacity problem. These are the systems collectors keep coming back to.

:::cards
### 12. Shallow bookcases with extra shelves
An 80 cm [IKEA Billy](https://www.ikea.com/ie/en/p/billy-bookcase-white-00263850/) is 28 cm deep, 202 cm tall, and ships with four adjustable shelves plus a fixed one. Add extra shelves to reach eight rows and collectors report about 400 DVDs or 450 Blu-rays per case.
### 13. Cube units, double-rowed
A Kallax compartment is 33 x 33 cm and 39 cm deep, so two rows of cases fit front to back. A 4x4 unit holds roughly 700 DVDs that way.
### 14. Floor-to-ceiling wall shelving
Shallow wall-mounted standards and brackets let you set every shelf exactly to case height and use the full wall.
### 15. Rotating media towers
A spinning tower packs a lot of discs into a small footprint. Check that it takes Blu-ray height and won't tip when fully loaded.
### 16. Sleeve binders for the archive
Moving rarely watched discs into soft sleeves frees most of the space. Keep the artwork, and read the risks of cheap plastic sleeves first.
:::

:::note Why a Kallax cube can't take two layers of Blu-rays
Two Blu-ray cases stacked upright need 34.3 cm. A Kallax compartment is 33 cm tall, so you get one layer per cube however you slice it. Double-row front to back instead.
:::

## What are some DIY DVD storage ideas?

DIY storage is where you can match shelf spacing to your cases exactly, which no off-the-shelf furniture does.

:::cards
### 17. Wooden crates
Wine or fruit crates turned on their side make modular cubes. Sand the inside and seal it so nothing snags the cases.
### 18. Rain gutter ledges
A classic children's book hack: short lengths of vinyl gutter screwed to the wall hold cases face out. Cheap, light and easy to reposition.
### 19. Dresser drawers, spines up
An old dresser becomes a filing cabinet for discs. Check the drawer height is over 19 cm for DVDs or about 17.5 cm for Blu-ray.
### 20. Built-ins under the stairs
The triangle under a staircase is ideal for shallow shelves at case height, because nothing else fits there anyway.
:::

## How do you find a disc once it's in storage?

This is the step every roundup skips. Double-rowed cubes, closed drawers, binders and under-bed boxes all hide spines. Without a system, you end up pulling out forty cases to find one film.

:::steps
### Label every storage spot
Give each unit, shelf, box or drawer a short code, such as *Living room · Cube C2* or *Closet · Box 3*. Small labels on shelf edges are enough.
### Put front rows and back rows to work
In double-row storage, keep favorites in front and the archive behind. Record which row each disc is in.
### Record the location in your catalog
Add the location to every entry, so searching for a title tells you the exact spot.
### Update it when discs move
Re-shelving is when catalogs drift. Change the location the same day you move a box.
:::

## Which DVD storage idea should you pick?

Run through this before you buy anything:

:::checklist Before you buy storage
- Count discs by format: DVD, Blu-ray, 4K and box sets
- Multiply by 20 per foot (DVD) or 23 (Blu-ray), then add 25%
- Measure the wall, alcove or closet you plan to use
- Check shelf openings: about 21 cm for DVD, 19 cm for Blu-ray
- Check depth: around 15 cm for one row, about 30 cm or more for two
- Decide which discs stay on display and which go to the archive
- Label every unit and record locations in your catalog
:::

## Count your discs, then pick your storage

Before you buy a single shelf, count what you own by format and work out the shelf length. The right idea usually becomes obvious: ledges for a few dozen, cubes for a few hundred, a wall of shallow shelving for the rest. Then label the spots and record them, so the collection stays as easy to search as it is to look at.
