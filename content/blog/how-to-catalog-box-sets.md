The cleanest way to catalog a box set is to **store it as one package and list every film inside it**. You scan the barcode on the outer box once, check that every film was picked up, and give the whole set one shelf address. That keeps your shelf, your film count and your search results in agreement, which is the whole point.

Box sets are where most catalogs go wrong. Collectors have argued for decades about whether a set counts as one title, four films or nine discs, and every option breaks something if you pick it without thinking. This guide shows you how to set a rule once and catalog every set, bonus disc and complete series the same way.

- 9 | discs in the 2003 Alien Quadrilogy DVD | for just 4 films

:::

**TL;DR**

1. One box set = one package in your catalog, with every film listed inside it.
2. Scan the barcode on the outer box, not the cases inside.
3. Count films for your stats. Count discs only if you need to.
4. Note bonus discs and what's on each disc.
5. Give the whole set one shelf address.
6. If you split a set or double-dip a film, keep separate entries and add a note.

## Should a box set count as one movie or several?

This is the question every collector hits first, and the answer depends on what you want your catalog to do. There are three common approaches, and each changes your numbers a lot. Take the [Alien Quadrilogy DVD set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alien_(franchise)) from 2003: one box, four films, nine discs.

:::bars One box set, three ways to count it (Alien Quadrilogy, 2003 DVD)
- Count the box | 1 | 1 entry
- Count the films | 4 | 4 films
- Count the discs | 9 | 9 discs
:::

Each method has a reason to exist:

:::cards
### Count the box
Matches what's physically on your shelf and keeps your total honest. But search for *Aliens* and you may get nothing, because the only entry is called *Alien Quadrilogy*.
### Count the films
Makes film counts, director totals and "have I seen this?" checks accurate. On its own, though, it loses the fact that the four films live in one box.
### Count the discs
Useful for insurance lists and for knowing how much space you need. It inflates your total with bonus discs, though, and says little about what you can actually watch.
### Do all three (recommended)
Store the box as one package, list the films inside it, and note the disc count. Your shelf, your stats and your search all stay right.
:::

:::pullquote
Catalog the box the way it sits on your shelf, and the films the way you search for them.
:::

The hybrid is what serious collectors have landed on. Collection apps have moved the same way: CLZ, for example, added a choice in 2018 to add a box set as a single entry or as separate movies, because users split hard on the question.

## How do you catalog a box set, step by step?

Once you've picked the hybrid rule, every set follows the same routine. It takes about a minute per box, even for big sets.

:::steps
### Scan the barcode on the outer box
The box or slipcase barcode identifies the set as a release. A four-film set usually has a different UPC from the four films sold on their own, so scanning the box is what records the edition you actually own.
### Check that every film was picked up
Open the entry and make sure each film inside is listed. Sets that bundle extras or a bonus feature are the ones most likely to be incomplete.
### Add the disc count and the bonus discs
Write down how many discs are in the box and what's on the extras, such as "Disc 9: Quadrilogy bonus disc". This is what you'll want when a disc goes missing.
### Record the edition details once, on the set
Format, region, packaging (digipak, slipcase, booklet) and where you bought it belong on the box entry, not copied onto every film.
### Give the whole set one shelf address
Box sets rarely fit the alphabetical run. A single spot like *Living room · Bottom shelf · Box sets* works better than scattering the films.
:::

## Which barcode do you scan on a box set?

Scan the outer box. That one rule fixes most box set mistakes.

| What's in your hand | What to scan | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Box with discs in a single digipak or fold-out | The barcode on the box | It's the only barcode, and it identifies the set |
| Slipcase holding separate retail cases | The slipcase barcode only | Scanning the inner cases too adds every film twice |
| Set with no barcode (shrink-wrap sticker gone) | Search by the set's title | Pick the matching release and year |
| Single disc with several films ("Double Feature") | The case barcode | Treat it as a small set with two films inside |
| Import set with a 13-digit EAN | The EAN, as normal | Barcode apps read UPC and EAN alike |

:::warning The double-scan trap
Sets like trilogy slipcases often hold full retail cases, each with its own barcode. If you scan the slipcase and then the cases inside, your catalog says you own the trilogy twice. Pick the outer box and stop there.
:::

## How do you catalog a complete series or TV box set?

TV sets follow the same logic, with one wrinkle: how you bought them.

:::versus Complete series box | Seasons bought separately
- One package, one barcode, one shelf address
- List the seasons inside it, like the films in a movie set
- Note the disc count, since TV sets run long
---
- One entry per season, because each has its own barcode
- Group them with the same shelf location so they stay together
- Track missing seasons on a wishlist, not as empty entries
:::

Either way, keep the seasons in order on the shelf and in the catalog. A note like "Season 3, disc 2 missing" is far more useful than discovering it halfway through a rewatch.

## What about bonus discs, combo packs and digital codes?

These are the extras that quietly break a catalog if you ignore them.

- **Bonus discs:** don't create a separate entry. Add them to the set's disc count and note what's on them.
- **4K combo packs:** a 4K set that also includes Blu-rays doubles the disc count. Record that the 1080p discs are there, since they're often what you lend out.
- **Digital codes:** note whether the code was redeemed. Unused codes inside old sets are easy to forget.
- **Booklets, art cards and posters:** list them in the packaging details. Collectors check for these when buying used sets, so they matter if you ever sell.

## What if you split a set or own a film twice?

Real collections are messy. You might sell one film out of a trilogy, or keep a standalone steelbook of a film that's also in a set. Here's how to keep the catalog honest.

### When you split a set

If you sell or give away part of a set, edit the set entry so it lists only what you still own, and add a note like "Disc 3 sold, Nov 2026". Don't delete the whole set and re-add the remaining films one by one, because you lose the edition details.

### When you double-dip

Owning a film both in a box set and on its own is common. The standalone release often has a newer transfer or different extras. The [Back to the Future trilogy](/blog/back-to-the-future-4k-vs-blu-ray/) is a classic case: its 2020 and 2025 4K sets share the same movie discs but come with different bonus discs. Keep both entries. Because your set lists every film inside it, searching for the title shows both copies, which is exactly what stops you buying a third.

## How do boutique and limited box sets change things?

Boutique labels make box sets that are part catalog entry, part artifact. The [Criterion Collection's Godzilla: The Showa-Era Films](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/6651-a-peek-inside-one-of-our-biggest-collectors-sets-ever) holds 15 films in a single hardcover book and took spine number 1000. For sets like this, a few extra fields are worth recording:

:::checklist Extra details for collector's box sets
- **Spine number or catalog number** for the set as a whole
- **Limited edition number** if the set is numbered
- **Packaging** such as hardcover book, rigid box or slipcase
- **Every insert**, from booklets to art cards
- **Condition of the outer box**, since corners and spines dent first
- **Purchase price**, because some sets go out of print and climb in value
:::

These details are the difference between "I own the Godzilla set" and a record you could hand to an insurer or a buyer. ## Start with your biggest set

Pull the box set you'd least want to lose a disc from, scan its outer barcode, check every film is listed and add the disc count. Once one set is done properly, the rest of the shelf takes minutes, and you'll never again wonder whether *Aliens* is in the collection or just in the box.
