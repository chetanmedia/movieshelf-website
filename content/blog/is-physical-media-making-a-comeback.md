Physical media is making a comeback among collectors, but not in the mass market. US disc spending is still shrinking, down 9.3% in 2025 to $870 million, yet that decline has slowed from more than 23% the year before, and 4K Blu-ray sales grew 12% in 2025 and 18.6% in the second quarter of 2026, according to the [Digital Entertainment Group](https://www.degonline.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/f2_DEGMidYear2026_covernote-grid_8.12.26.pdf). The honest answer is: fewer people buy discs, but the people who do are buying the best ones.

Plenty of articles say "physical media is back" and stop there. This one shows the actual numbers, explains why they look contradictory, and what it means if you collect.

:::stats
- +12% | 4K Blu-ray sales growth in 2025 | DEG full-year report
- +18.6% | 4K UHD spending growth in Q2 2026 | DEG mid-year report
- −9.3% | total US disc spending in 2025 | down from −23.4% in 2024
- $870M | US disc sales in 2025 | vs $6.1B in 2015
:::

<div class="tldr">

**TL;DR**

- **Overall disc sales are still falling**, but far more slowly: −23.4% in 2024, −9.3% in 2025, −5.2% in Q2 2026.
- **Premium discs are growing.** 4K UHD rose 12% in 2025, and total Blu-ray spending grew 5.5% in Q2 2026.
- **Streaming still dominates**, at more than 90% of US home entertainment spending.
- **It's a collector comeback**, driven by 4K, steelbooks and boutique labels, plus used discs the sales data doesn't count.

</div>

## What do the sales numbers actually say?

The Digital Entertainment Group (DEG) publishes the most widely cited US figures every quarter, using retail data. Here's the recent trend for disc sell-through spending (DVD, Blu-ray and 4K UHD combined):

| Period | Disc spending change | 4K UHD | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 full year | −23.4% | not broken out | DEG via [What Hi-Fi?](https://www.whathifi.com/us/streaming-entertainment/streaming-remains-on-top-but-4k-blu-ray-is-making-a-comeback) |
| Q3 2025 | −2.9% | +30% or more | DEG Q3 2025 |
| 2025 full year | −9.3% ($870M) | +12% | DEG full-year 2025 |
| Q1 2026 | −8.4% ($185M) | Blu-ray and 4K each down under 5% | DEG Q1 2026 |
| Q2 2026 | −5.2% ($176M) | +18.6% | DEG mid-year 2026 |

The direction is the story. For most of the 2020s, disc spending fell by 20 to 25% a year, according to [Luminate's analysis of DEG data](https://luminatedata.com/blog/is-dvd-the-new-vinyl-the-silver-lining-to-spiraling-disc-spend/). That fall has now slowed to single digits, and in the second quarter of 2026 total Blu-ray spending actually rose, by 5.5%, because 4K UHD grew fast enough to offset everything else.

:::bars How fast US disc spending is falling (year-over-year decline)
- 2024 | 23.4 | −23.4%
- 2025 | 9.3 | −9.3%
- First half of 2026 | 6.9 | −6.9%
- Q2 2026 | 5.2 | −5.2%
:::

:::note Why "comeback" and "decline" are both true
Total disc sales are a mix of cheap DVDs, which are collapsing, and premium 4K and collector releases, which are growing. When the cheap part shrinks and the premium part grows, the total keeps falling while the collector market gets healthier.
:::

## Is physical media dying, then?

As a mass-market product, it already has. In 2015 US disc sales were worth $6.1 billion. In 2025 they were $870 million, roughly 1.4% of all home entertainment spending, while subscription streaming made up 92.4%. Most households no longer buy discs at all.

The retail picture reflects that. Netflix shut down its DVD-by-mail service in September 2023, and [Best Buy stopped selling movies on disc in 2024](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/best-buy-dvd-blu-ray-discs-stop-selling), saying "the way we watch movies and TV shows is much different today." Walk into a big-box store and the disc aisle is a fraction of what it was.

:::versus What's shrinking | What's growing
- Cheap new-release DVDs
- Big-box retail shelf space
- Disc rental (the DEG stopped tracking it in 2023)
- Casual, one-time-watch purchases
---
- 4K UHD Blu-ray
- Steelbooks and collector's editions
- Boutique labels restoring catalog films
- Used discs, which the sales figures don't count
:::

So physical media is not dying for collectors. It's turning into something closer to vinyl: smaller, more expensive per item, and bought deliberately by people who care about the format.

## Why are people going back to physical media?

The reasons come up again and again on r/4kbluray, r/bluray and r/dvdcollection, and most of them are about control rather than nostalgia.

:::cards
### Films keep disappearing from streaming
Licensing deals expire and titles vanish, sometimes from every service at once. A disc stays on your shelf regardless.
### Digital "purchases" are licenses
Most digital storefronts sell you a license that can be withdrawn. A disc can't be revoked by a rights change.
### Better picture and sound
A 4K Blu-ray carries far more video data than a typical 4K stream, usually with lossless audio. We compare them in [physical media vs streaming](/blog/physical-media-vs-streaming/).
### Subscription fatigue
Streaming prices keep rising and catalogs keep splitting across more services. Buying a disc of a film you rewatch every year can work out cheaper.
### Extras and packaging
Commentaries, documentaries, booklets and [steelbooks](/blog/what-is-a-steelbook/) are things streaming simply doesn't offer.
### Collecting is fun
Hunting for an edition, browsing a shelf and lending a favorite to a friend are part of the hobby.
:::

The DEG itself credits collectors. Its Q3 2025 report pointed to "continued strong demand for collectible titles including premium 4K Steelbook releases," and its 2026 reports say collectors "continue to show interest in premium formats."

:::pullquote
Fewer people buy discs now, but the people who do are buying the best ones.
:::

## Who is driving the physical media comeback?

Three groups show up most clearly.

**Home theater enthusiasts** buy 4K UHD for the picture and sound. If you've invested in an OLED TV and a Dolby Atmos system, the gap between a disc and a stream is easy to see and hear. Our guide to [4K Blu-ray vs Blu-ray](/blog/4k-blu-ray-vs-blu-ray/) covers when the upgrade is worth it.

**Boutique-label collectors** follow labels such as Criterion, Arrow, Kino Lorber, Shout! Factory and Vinegar Syndrome, which restore catalog and cult films that often aren't streaming anywhere. Their limited editions frequently sell out.

**Younger buyers** are a frequent theme in coverage of the trend, with thrifting and used-disc hunting as an entry point. There's no hard sales data on age, so treat this as a widely reported pattern rather than a measured one.

:::warning The data has a blind spot
The DEG tracks new disc sales through retail. It doesn't count used discs from thrift stores, garage sales, pawn shops or eBay, which is exactly where many new collectors start. The real level of interest is likely higher than the sales figures suggest.
:::

## What does the comeback mean for collectors?

A smaller, collector-focused market changes how you should buy. Limited runs sell out, prices are higher per disc, and fewer stores stock discs at all. A few habits help:

:::steps
### Buy the editions you'll keep
Prioritize 4K UHD and boutique releases of films you rewatch. That's what's still being made, and what tends to hold value.
### Don't wait on limited editions
Steelbooks and boutique limited editions often go out of print quickly. If a release matters to you, buy it early or at the next big sale.
### Check the used market
Thrift stores and secondhand shops are full of Blu-rays people sold off when they switched to streaming. It's the cheapest way to build a collection.
### Keep track of what you own
The more editions you buy, the easier it is to buy the same film twice. A catalog you can check on your phone in a store solves that.
:::

## Will physical media keep growing?

Nobody can say for certain, and anyone who promises a vinyl-style boom is guessing. What the data supports is narrower:

- **The decline is slowing.** Each recent DEG report has shown a smaller drop than the one before.
- **Premium formats are growing.** 4K UHD has posted growth in several recent quarters.
- **The mass market isn't coming back.** Streaming's share of spending is still above 90% and still rising.

There are also supply-side risks. Only a few mainstream brands still make dedicated 4K Blu-ray players, and studios release fewer new titles on disc in some markets. If you collect, the best response is simply to buy what you want while it's available, and look after it well.

:::checklist Collecting smart in a shrinking market
- Prioritize 4K UHD and boutique editions of films you rewatch
- Pre-order or buy limited editions early
- Check thrift stores and used shops for cheap Blu-rays
- Store discs upright, away from heat and sunlight
- Keep a catalog of every edition you own
- Keep a wishlist of the specific editions you're hunting
:::

## Physical media isn't back for everyone, but it is back for collectors

If you love films on disc, you're not holding on to a dead format. You're part of a smaller, more deliberate market that's buying better editions than ever. Make the most of it: buy the editions you'll keep, hunt the used bins, and know exactly what's already on your shelf.
